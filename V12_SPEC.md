# V12 — The Other Model

**Self-based maker inversion, projection correction, active interrogation, and selective uptake**

**Status:** implementation and execution specification
**Prepared:** 2026-08-24
**Repository reviewed:** main at 7e154bea5a7f1c4cf2d334185732136214ab62eb
**Execution class:** constructed-world research; CPU-first; no human or language-model evidence
**Relationship to V11:** V11 remains unchanged. V12 imports or independently reconstructs V11
primitives, reproduces the numbers it relies on, and answers the debts V11 explicitly left open.

This file is written before V12 implementation and before any V12 result is seen. It is the
top-level handoff requested by the curator. The coding agent may translate it into executable

## 0. Executive command

Build and run V12 as a broad constructed-world program around one central question:

> When does reusing one's own generative organization make another maker easier to invert, when
> does it create projection, and can evidence, context, and active probing correct the error?

The central object is no longer only a maker profile. It is a relation between:

- a maker with a persistent profile, expertise, process dynamics, and communicative regime;
- a reader with its own measured production model;
- the distance and structural correspondence between those models;
- the evidence the artifact and context supply;
- the reader's policy for inspecting, querying, stopping, and later using what it inferred.

V12 must keep three outputs separate:

1. **Reconstruction:** what the reader believes about the maker.
2. **Reliability and trust:** how much evidential weight the reader assigns that reconstruction.
3. **Uptake:** how, if at all, the inferred maker profile changes the reader's own preferred
   outcomes or later policy.

The program has eight substantive trunks:

| Trunk | Plain question |
|---|---|
| S — self and similarity | Does a self-derived prior improve inversion selectively for relevantly similar makers? |
| Q — active interrogation | Can the reader choose commissions or observations that efficiently distinguish makers? |
| B — bard and concealer | When does assuming that a maker intends to be understood help, and when is it exploitable? |
| U — uptake bridge | What happens when a calibrated maker posterior is allowed to affect later policy? |
| R — residue and preference | Can stable preference be separated from expertise and habit and predict another context? |
| T — coupling and supply | Which supplied latent variables unlock which other inferences? |
| D — director topology | Can broad causal reach be distinguished from distributed coherence and local implementation? |
| F — flattened intent | Can layered and flattened decision organization be distinguished at matched surface and density? |

The integrity trunk I and adversarial matrix X apply across them. All eight substantive roots must
be attempted. A failure in one does not stop unrelated trunks. A branch may close after its
instrument fails twice or its predeclared scientific closure lands; the program may not call
itself complete after running only the easiest spine.

This is not a duration contest. Use spare CPU because the questions warrant it, not to manufacture
a week of wall time. If the named program runs faster than expected, expand independent worlds,
makers, domains, and confirmation draws in the frozen order in §17. Do not duplicate nearly
identical rollouts merely to keep cores busy.

## 1. What V12 can and cannot establish

### 1.1 Claim ceiling

V12 can establish:

- whether a specified self-to-other inference architecture is logically sufficient for an
  efficiency, projection, and correction pattern;
- the sample and compute price of that pattern inside randomized constructed worlds;
- whether an estimator can recover a planted latent, residual, topology, or preference;
- whether active information-seeking and downstream policy selection behave as predicted under
  exact ground truth;
- which assumptions and solver approximations carry each result.

V12 cannot establish:

- that people use this mechanism;
- that a PyMDP agent has empathy, affect, a limbic system, or a midbrain analogue;
- that a model-family effect in Sounding Line is explained by the V12 construction;
- that the four V11 goal channels are human values;
- that artifact-only reconstruction identifies a maker's real history where multiple histories
  are observationally equivalent;
- that a simulated uptake effect is human indoctrination or alignment.

Every verdict must state whether it is:

- **METHOD:** a ruler result expected to transfer because the answer was planted;
- **CONSTRUCTED MECHANISM:** a consequence of the declared world;
- **BOUNDARY:** a demonstrated non-identifiability or failure under the tested family;
- **INSTRUMENT FAILURE:** the question was not answered;
- **VOID:** the manipulation did not reach the emitter or the target did not exist.

### 1.2 The distinctive hypothesis

Ordinary Bayesian inverse planning already provides artifact-to-maker inference. The distinctive
V12 hypothesis is narrower:

> A reader can initialize another-maker inference from its own production model. Relevant
> similarity makes that shortcut efficient. Self–other distinction and target evidence must then
> correct projection when the maker differs.

A close reader merely outperforming a distant reader is not enough. A reader handed the correct
likelihood family has simply been given more information. V12 must compare self-based inference
against controls matched for prior entropy, expected distance to truth, compute, and evidence.

The target pattern is a conjunction:

1. self-first improves proper score or reduces evidence cost for nearby makers;
2. the advantage is selective rather than a universal prompt or compute benefit;
3. self-first initially harms or supplies no benefit for conflict makers;
4. diagnostic evidence corrects the error while retaining self and other as separate models;
5. the corrected model predicts an unseen continuation or chooses a better probe.

### 1.3 Why the newest active-inference work does not settle this

Recent active-inference work now uses structurally matched self and other models. Albarracin et al.
separate opponent prediction from an exogenous other-regarding weight and report that opponent
beliefs can converge without producing cooperation. Pitliya et al. maintain distinct models of
self and other and identify heterogeneous models, learning, adversarial interaction, and
quantitative robustness as open problems.

Those papers make V12 timely, not redundant. They also impose a design correction: **accurately
reconstructing another agent and caring about or adopting its outcomes are different
computations.** V12 therefore never names an uptake weight “empathy.”

## 3. V12 world

### 3.1 Latent objects

Use separate names and factors for separate claims:

| Symbol | Object | Timescale |
|---|---|---|
| g | current proximal goal | one episode or artifact |
| w | persistent maker profile over goal channels | many artifacts |
| k | expertise or mechanic: the maker's transition/emission competence | many episodes, domain-local |
| h | habit: repeated, potentially goal-derived implementation tendency | many episodes |
| r | communicative regime: cooperative, neutral, or concealing | source or episode |
| c_context | commission, tools, constraints, source history, and available alternatives | episode |
| M_self | reader's measured production model | reader |
| M_other | reader's posterior model of the maker | reader × maker |
| C_AIF | active-inference preferred outcomes used for reader policy | task and uptake phase |

Never use bare C in prose. Context and active-inference preference are different objects.

### 3.2 Self and other models

Represent a production model as a family of observation mappings, transition mappings, priors, and
standing profile parameters. The self-model must be behaviorally instantiated: before reading
another maker, the reader produces or chooses in the same controlled environments. Its measured
self-policy is the basis of M_self.

M_other may begin from:

1. a transformation of M_self;
2. a population prior;
3. a generic prior matched to the self prior's information;
4. a deliberately mismatched self prior;
5. an oracle model used only as a ceiling.

Self-first means estimating a difference from a measured self-model. It does not mean inserting a
sentence saying “imagine what you would do.”

### 3.3 Similarity axes

Do not collapse similarity into one scalar until the components have been manipulated separately.
Construct at least these axes:

- **structural similarity:** same factorization and state correspondence;
- **observation similarity:** related A-like feature likelihoods;
- **process similarity:** related B-like transition or execution dynamics;
- **profile similarity:** related persistent w;
- **prior similarity:** related D-like base rates;
- **policy similarity:** similar choices after all components interact;
- **surface/source similarity:** a nuisance channel that looks alike without sharing policy;
- **irrelevant similarity:** shared dimensions unrelated to the target;
- **anti-similarity:** a systematic transform that makes self a misleading starting point.

Orthogonalize these where possible. At minimum, policy similarity must be crossed with
surface/source similarity, and process similarity must be crossed with profile similarity.

### 3.4 Information-matched controls

The central threat is trivial prior advantage. For every self-first comparison, construct a generic
prior with:

- the same entropy;
- the same expected KL or Jensen–Shannon distance to the truth over the sampled world family;
- the same number of free parameters;
- the same evidence and policy-evaluation budget;
- no privileged self-to-maker coordinate correspondence.

Also include:

- a permuted-self prior preserving every marginal while destroying correspondence;
- a random local prior at the same distance;
- a direct maker prior where appropriate;
- a compute-matched generic deliberation path.

If exact information matching is impossible, report the remaining mismatch and bound how large a
performance difference it can explain before interpreting the self route.

### 3.5 Artifact domains

Use at least two abstract domains with the same latent decision topology and different surface
conventions. Suggested domains:

- sequential construction: choose a goal, order operations, accept tradeoffs;
- editorial composition: choose section order, emphasis, repair, and final veto;
- resource design: trade robustness, cost, speed, and interpretability.

Do not call any domain “art” merely because the theory concerns art. The construction needs
known alternatives and causal histories, not decorative semantics.

### 3.6 Opportunity records

Every generated decision must store:

- alternatives available at that moment;
- their utilities or predicted outcomes under the maker model;
- the selected action;
- upstream dependencies;
- whether the action was deliberated, habitual, delegated, ratified, or unnoticed;
- which portions reached the final artifact;
- the random seed and every model parameter needed to reproduce it.

Values and preferences are inferred from opportunities, not from action counts alone. The same
choice can be weak or strong evidence depending on what it cost the maker to select it.

## 4. Solver architecture and the proper role of PyMDP

### 4.1 Three paths

Every central result uses, where representable:

1. **Exact enumerative or closed-form inference** as the reference answer.
2. **The repository's pinned legacy PyMDP agent** as the active reader.
3. **A simple non-maker baseline** such as frequency, surface classification, or direct
   conditional likelihood.

The exact path decides identifiability. PyMDP supplies bounded attention, epistemic action,
inspection cost, stopping, and downstream policy. A mean-field approximation may be a model of a
limited reader; it may not silently become the ground truth.

### 4.2 Do not migrate the closed environment

The repository pins inferactively-pymdp 1.0.2 and imports pymdp.legacy. V12 must not migrate the
project to the modern JAX API or change the core dependency contract. If a modern JAX comparison
is judged useful:

- put it behind a new optional extra;
- keep it in an isolated V12 module;
- force CPU execution;
- reproduce an exact and legacy anchor first;
- record it as a solver comparison, never a replacement.

Modern PyMDP offers batching, parameter information gain, sophisticated inference, and MCTS, but
none of those features is required to answer V12. Implement the exact finite information-value
calculation first. Use library machinery only when it reproduces the known answer.

### 4.3 Active reader factors

The PyMDP reader should minimally represent:

- belief over candidate maker profiles or types;
- belief over communicative regime where relevant;
- evidence-source or context reliability where relevant;
- controllable attention/probe choice;
- observation and integration costs;
- stop/abstain action;
- downstream task action in the uptake phase.

The reader has zero preference over provenance labels, maker identity, or being correct about a
named profile. Its preferences concern task outcomes and explicit inspection/integration cost.
Add construction assertions equivalent to N7.

### 4.4 Solver discrepancy rule

For every primary contrast report:

- exact result;
- legacy PyMDP result;
- direction agreement;
- magnitude ratio;
- policy disagreement rate;
- whether either verdict changes.

If a headline changes direction, exact arithmetic owns the identifiability statement and the
PyMDP result becomes a bounded-reader failure. Do not average the solvers.

Every named card below is either mandatory, conditional with an explicit trigger, or explicitly
deferred. No card is “optional.”

- independent unit;

- factor levels and sample floors;
- exact, PyMDP, and baseline paths required;
- live, positive, placebo, identity, and no-oracle gates;
- primary estimand;
- null and alternative expectations;
- strongest rival;
- output path and completion marker;
- estimated and actual CPU minutes;
- closure or resource-block reason.

Allowed states:

- PLANNED
- BUILT
- INSTRUMENT_VALID
- RUNNING
- LANDED
- INSTRUMENT_FAILED
- SCIENTIFIC_CLOSED
- RESOURCE_BLOCKED

DONE is forbidden.

### 5.1 Minimum independent information

Unless a card specifies more:

- at least three ordinary stochastic seeds per condition;
- at least twelve independent randomized world constructions in discovery;
- at least twelve untouched world constructions in confirmation for a promoted result;
- at least sixty maker instances per primary profile or regime comparison;
- artifact prefixes at 1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 20, and 50 where convergence is claimed;
- artifact lengths at 2, 4, 8, 12, and 24 observations where saturation is relevant;
- two domains before claiming a domain-general shape.

The independent unit is world or maker, not artifact rows generated by one maker. Use hierarchical
bootstrap, cluster permutation, or world-level aggregation as appropriate.

### 5.2 One-repair rule

An instrument gets one predeclared construction repair after a failed known-answer gate. Preserve
the first attempt and its failure. If the repaired instrument fails, close that reader or
measurement family. Continue unrelated trunks.

If a downstream card remains methodologically informative, feed it the exact or oracle upstream
posterior and label the result as a method ceiling. This prevents one failed reader from cancelling
the entire forest.

## 6. Trunk I — integrity, severity, and solver controls

All I cards are mandatory and run before their dependent interpretations.

### I01. V11 reproduction anchor

Reproduce every quantity in §2.3 from reconstructed inner loops without calling a V11 run.

**Pass:** all scientific fields within frozen numerical tolerance.
**Failure:** stop V11-dependent V12 scoring until the mismatch is explained. Do not rewrite the
tolerance after inspection.

### I02. SV-T randomized severity

Randomize within declared broad but valid ranges:

- goal-signature separation;
- structured synthetic distribution;
- tier transmission;
- profile concentration and family size;
- maker count, artifact count, and observation length;
- reader signature corruption;
- commission amplification and compliance share;
- noise and goal base rates.

Run at least fifty valid architecture draws. Report reproduction surfaces for S-14 and S-15, not
one survival percentage. Identify which parameters and interactions carry each result using a
Morris screen followed by Sobol analysis for the small surviving set.

**Meaning:** method/architecture boundary only. No human mechanism claim.

### I03. Exact–PyMDP calibration triangle

Create small worlds where the exact posterior and optimal information-seeking policy can be
enumerated. Compare:

- exact joint-state inference;
- legacy PyMDP mean-field inference;
- a deliberately factorized approximation;
- random action.

Sweep coupling strength among hidden factors. Locate the region in which mean-field becomes
confidently wrong.

**Gate:** all solvers agree in a factor-independent identity world.
**Output:** solver-discrepancy phase diagram used to qualify every later PyMDP result.

### I04. No-information, shuffled, and false-correspondence nulls

For every reader family:

- remove maker dependence from emissions;
- shuffle artifacts across makers;
- permute self-to-maker coordinates;
- preserve source style while shuffling policy;
- preserve policy while shuffling source style.

The first three must return prior-calibrated uncertainty. The last two establish whether policy or
surface correspondence carries the result.

### I05. Realization and opportunity gate

For every maker generator, verify:

- assigned profile changed actual choices;
- every stated alternative was genuinely available;
- downstream artifacts differ when a supposedly causal upstream variable changes;
- matched arms actually match their declared marginals;
- hidden continuations were not exposed to the reader.

A failed realization makes the card VOID, not negative.

### I06. Reproducibility and independence

Assert:

- all seeds derive from stable named CRC32 strings, never Python hash;
- discovery and confirmation world lineages never overlap;
- artifact descendants remain on one side of the split;
- cached outputs include the card and source hashes;
- per-rollout files use the points naming convention and remain ignored;
- aggregate summaries alone are committed.

## 7. Trunk S — self, similarity, projection, and correction

All S cards are mandatory.

### S01. Measure the reader's self-model

Have each reader produce choices and artifacts in both domains before it observes any maker.
Estimate its own profile, process transitions, habits, and uncertainty.

**Known answer:** directly recorded self choices.
**Gate:** self-model predicts held-out self actions above population and frequency baselines and
remains stable across paraphrased surface conventions.
**Closure:** if self policies are unstable or identical, replace the decision environment once.
If still unstable, this construction cannot test self-based inversion.

### S02. Similarity ruler

Build maker–reader pairs varying one similarity axis at a time while holding the others fixed.
Verify measured distance recovers the planted ordering and does not merely identify source labels.

Run:

- A-like observation match only;
- B-like process match only;
- profile match only;
- prior match only;
- policy match with different source;
- source match with different policy;
- irrelevant match;
- anti-similar transform.

**Output:** a distance matrix and uncertainty, not a single hand-designed similarity number.

### S03. Off-ceiling expertise price

This is T-11's debt. Cross reader expertise corruption with artifact length, transmission, noise,
and goal separation. Locate cells where the exact expert is neither at ceiling nor floor.

Primary questions:

- Does expertise reduce the number of observations needed to identify current goals?
- Does that improvement propagate into profile recovery?
- Does it improve calibration or only top choice?

The original V11 C2 failure remains a failure. V12 reports a new result under new conditions.

### S04. Self-first versus information-matched generic prior

Compare:

1. self-first transformation;
2. population prior;
3. information-matched generic prior;
4. permuted self;
5. random local prior;
6. oracle other-model.

Hold compute and evidence fixed. Score maker-profile posterior and hidden next choice.

**Primary estimand:** self-first proper-score gain over the information-matched generic prior as a
function of planted self–maker distance.
**Rival:** trivial closeness or coordinate privilege.

### S05. Distance × evidence phase diagram

Cross at least five self–maker distance levels with artifact evidence dose. Measure:

- gain at the first evidence point;
- evidence required to reach a fixed proper score;
- final calibration;
- asymptotic error;
- error directed toward the reader's own policy.

The predicted surface is helpful near self, neutral or harmful far away, with declining
self-directed error as target evidence accumulates.

### S06. Diagnostic conflict and correction

Begin with evidence compatible with self, then reveal maker-specific counterevidence. Reverse the
order in matched episodes. Include weak and decisive conflict.

Report:

- anchoring strength;
- correction slope and half-life;
- residual self-directed bias;
- confidence during conflict;
- explicit probability on an unresolved/equifinal state.

Immediate confidence reversal without evidence sensitivity is prompt following. Failure to correct
is projection.

### S07. Hidden continuation

Reveal a maker's profile-building history but hide the next:

- repair;
- route choice;
- commissioned response;
- tradeoff;
- abstention or acceptance.

Retrospective profile fit is insufficient. The self route must improve a proper score on the hidden
continuation beyond generic and direct-frequency baselines.

### S08. Fresh maker and fresh domain transfer

Freeze every setting selected in S01–S07. Test on:

- untouched maker instances;
- a second surface domain;
- a new profile mixture;
- a coordinate-relabelled world;
- an anti-similar maker family.

No cross-domain claim survives without this card.

### S09. Calibration and selective refusal

Allow readers to abstain. Plot risk against coverage across similarity and evidence. Test whether
self-first improves:

- accuracy at matched coverage;
- recognition of underdetermination;
- refusal under equifinality;
- confidence after correction.

A reader that gains top-1 accuracy by answering everything more confidently has not earned a
better maker model.

### S10. Similarity decomposition verdict

Fit a predeclared hierarchical analysis over the full S matrix. Compare worlds:

- shared policy;
- shared process;
- family/source dialect analogue;
- generic reader quality;
- irrelevant similarity;
- projection.

Do not average over reversals. Report the whole maker × reader × axis matrix and which distances
retain independent predictive value.

**S closure:** close this engineered self-first route if it never beats the information- and
compute-matched generic prior, does not interact with measured similarity, and worsens correction
or calibration after one valid repair. This weakens the implementation, not human simulation
theory.

## 8. Trunk Q — active interrogation and epistemic foraging

All Q cards are mandatory. This is where PyMDP should add something that exact passive inference
does not.

### Q01. Exact information-value positive control

Give the reader actions with analytically known expected information gain over maker types.
Require exact ranking of probes, then require the PyMDP policy to prefer the most informative probe
when costs are equal.

Controls:

- random probe;
- utility-only agent with information gain disabled;
- state-information gain;
- parameter-information gain where supported;
- oracle one-step lookahead.

### Q02. Commission selection

Extend S-14. The reader may commission one of several tasks, each targeting a different possible
drive. Some commissions are diagnostic only if a drive is absent; others are redundant.

Ask whether the reader chooses the commission that best distinguishes its current hypotheses and
whether that advantage survives probe cost.

### Q03. Episode selection

Give six prior maker episodes and a budget to inspect two before predicting a seventh. Compare:

- self-first active selection;
- target-only uncertainty sampling;
- population-prior selection;
- random selection;
- oracle best pair.

Score both information captured and final hidden-continuation prediction.

### Q04. Context-source selection

Offer artifact evidence, biography, prior work, tool records, and source reputation at different
costs and reliabilities. The reader chooses what to inspect.

True and false context are externally controlled. A useful reader should buy context when it is
expected to discriminate makers, not whenever it is polished or confident.

### Q05. Cost and stopping frontier

Sweep inspection cost, integration cost, and evidence quality. Report:

- information acquired per unit cost;
- error at stopping;
- unnecessary inspection;
- premature stopping;
- regret against the exact optimal policy.

This is a risk–coverage and resource-allocation result, not a metabolic measurement.

### Q06. Adversarial probe environment

Some probes are selected by a concealer that anticipates the reader. Compare myopic and
two-step/sophisticated policies if tractable. Determine whether active selection still helps or
whether it predictably walks into planted evidence.

**Q closure:** close active self-based probing if it never beats ordinary uncertainty sampling on
information or final prediction and its extra cost is not recovered. Retain any general
information-seeking result without a self-route interpretation.

## 9. Trunk B — bard, neutral maker, and concealer

All B cards are mandatory. The label “bard” means a maker that selects traces to aid a reader; it
does not imply artistic quality.

### B01. Regime construction and surface matching

Create three maker regimes:

- **bard:** chooses among goal-equivalent realizations to make its true profile easier to infer;
- **neutral:** optimizes only the artifact task;
- **concealer:** chooses among goal-equivalent realizations to make a false profile likely.

Match:

- task success;
- surface entropy and feature frequency;
- artifact length;
- effort or action count;
- current goal;
- available realization set.

The regime manipulation must change inferential correspondence without changing the named cheap
features.

### B02. Reader assumption factorial

Cross maker regime with readers assuming:

- cooperative teaching;
- neutral sampling;
- adversarial selection;
- uncertain regime with a prior over all three.

Measure sample efficiency, calibration, and confident error. The cooperative assumption should
help on bards and expose a cost on concealers. A universal benefit indicates an easier likelihood,
not a communicative assumption.

### B03. Learn the source regime

Expose repeated source histories and let the reader update belief over regime. Cross source
identity with current artifact evidence so that “who usually teaches” and “what this artifact
supports” sometimes disagree.

The reader must distinguish source history from content and recover after regime changes.

### B04. Active challenge

Let the reader choose a challenge or commission designed to separate honest scaffolding from
strategic shaping. Compare passive reading, random challenge, uncertainty sampling, and
regime-aware active selection.

### B05. Partial and bounded concealment

Do not use only exact negation, which earlier work found easy to detect. Sweep:

- subtle deflection;
- selective omission;
- surface mimicry;
- one false high-level cue with truthful local process;
- truthful high-level goal with misleading implementation;
- adaptive concealment that spends a bounded budget.

Report where concealment is unreadable, where effort spent hiding becomes the signal, and where the
reader becomes confidently wrong.

### B06. Exploitation after accurate reconstruction

Give the reader an accurate model of a low-cooperation or adversarial maker, then distinguish:

- prediction accuracy;
- trust;
- willingness to adopt;
- own payoff;
- maker payoff.

This card guards against equating “understands the other” with “coordinates” or “cares.” Accurate
inference may improve exploitation or self-protection rather than cooperation.

**B closure:** if regime cannot be varied without an unmatched surface channel after one rebuild,
mark the artifact-only question VOID. A records-aware regime reader may still serve as a ceiling.

## 10. Trunk U — from maker posterior to preferred outcomes and policy

All U cards are mandatory. If S readers fail, use exact and deliberately corrupted posteriors so
the bridge itself is still testable.

### U01. Bridge identity and no-change gates

Implement the explicit bridge:

\[
q(w_{\mathrm{maker}}\mid a_{1:n})
\rightarrow
q_{\mathrm{trusted}}(w)
\rightarrow
C_{\mathrm{AIF}}
\rightarrow
\pi_{\mathrm{reader}}.
\]

Required identities:

- zero uptake weight leaves reader policy bit-identical;
- oracle correct posterior at full uptake moves policy in the intended direction;
- uniform posterior produces no directional update;
- shuffled maker posterior does not improve the target task;
- C_AIF contains no provenance or source-label preference.

### U02. Reconstruction accuracy × uptake weight

Orthogonally vary posterior accuracy and uptake weight. Include:

- accurate and confident;
- accurate and uncertain;
- wrong and confident;
- wrong and uncertain;
- equifinal;
- no update.

This separates information quality from motivational weighting. Report policy behavior, not only
distance between vectors.

### U03. Posterior representation tournament

Compare:

- maximum a posteriori maker;
- posterior-mean profile;
- posterior predictive mixture;
- risk-sensitive lower-confidence update;
- confidence-gated update;
- oracle;
- no update.

Score downstream regret, calibration, and catastrophic wrong-direction movement. Full uncertainty
should matter most where histories remain equivalent.

### U04. Competence, relevance, reliability, and value similarity

Cross:

- maker competence;
- source reliability;
- relevance of maker expertise to the new task;
- maker–reader preference similarity;
- reconstruction accuracy.

No factor may be substituted for another. A competent maker can have divergent values; a similar
maker can be incompetent; a reliable source can report an irrelevant preference.

### U05. Process, belief, preference, and imitation

After exposure, test separately:

- reuse of an efficient process;
- acceptance of a factual belief;
- movement on a standing tradeoff;
- surface imitation;
- action under a novel constraint.

The result must state which channel moved. One aggregate “uptake” score is forbidden.

### U06. Negative transfer and exploitation

Include:

- competent but value-divergent maker;
- incompetent but value-similar maker;
- concealer with correct local technique;
- false context attached to a useful process;
- asymmetric other-regarding weight.

Measure when understanding improves prediction while worsening the reader's own task or making it
exploitable.

### U07. Correction and reversibility

After an update, reveal reliable counterevidence. Test whether the reader:

- revises its maker model;
- reverses inappropriate preference movement;
- retains useful process learning;
- remains anchored to a polished false source;
- distinguishes a corrected belief from a changed preference.

### U08. Repeated small updates

Run longitudinal streams with small per-episode updates and later maker differentiation. Compare
early versus late reliable context, intermittent conflict, and source switching. Report cumulative
policy movement and reversal, while calling it a constructed accumulation analogue rather than a
lifetime human effect.

**U closure:** if the bridge changes policy only because the target preference is directly written
into C_AIF, without dependence on calibrated reconstruction, the proposed inference-to-uptake
mechanism has not been tested. Keep the policy identity as a unit test and close the interpretive
branch.

## 11. Trunk R — habit, expertise, residuals, and standing preference

All R cards are mandatory.

### R01. Orthogonal planted world

Build an emitter with independently manipulable:

- current goal;
- domain expertise;
- three recurring reader-directed habits;
- persistent standing profile;
- source style;
- temporary state;
- context constraints.

Use a factorial or balanced fractional-factorial design that makes each latent separately
recoverable from records. Verify that no latent is a deterministic coarsening of another.

### R02. Residual estimator duel

This is T-13/S-16. Recover the planted standing profile using:

1. linear partialling-out;
2. constrained inverse planning or inverse reinforcement learning;
3. repetition-matched habit removal;
4. hierarchical latent model;
5. direct predictive model with no residual language;
6. oracle nuisance removal.

Score profile recovery and, more importantly, held-out choice prediction. Explicitly test whether
linear partialling deletes genuine signal or leaves habit shadows.

### R03. Nonlinear and interacting habits

Make habits depend on goal, expertise, and context. Include automatic choices that originated from
earlier preferences but now execute without deliberation.

This tests whether “subtract expertise, then read values” fails when expertise partly stores prior
values.

### R04. Cross-domain separator

Infer in domain one and predict structurally equivalent choices in domain two. Surface style and
implementation habits must change; the standing tradeoff remains.

An estimator that identifies makers in-domain but fails cross-domain is reading identity or habit,
not the planted persistent preference.

### R05. Opportunity-strength ruler

Vary the cost of choosing the preferred option. The same action under a near tie is weak evidence;
the action under a large opposing cost is strong evidence.

Require the recovered posterior to reflect opportunity strength rather than raw frequency.

### R06. Current goal × standing profile

Fully cross current proximal goal and persistent profile, including aligned and opposed cases.
Predict both jointly and score interaction. This is the values vertex needed for the actual triple
inference.

### R07. Prospective and counterfactual choice

Infer a profile from previous artifacts, then predict:

- a new action under changed cost;
- a new domain;
- a commission targeting an absent or weak drive;
- a counterfactual choice under a different current goal.

Retrospective fit without this card is not a value result.

### R08. Identifiability boundary

Construct pairs of distinct standing profiles that induce the same observed choices under a
restricted opportunity set. Confirm that artifact-only readers abstain while opportunity records
separate the profiles when they legitimately can.

**R closure:** close any estimator that fails held-out cross-context prediction after its one
repair, even if it classifies maker identity. Preserve local regularity without calling it value.

## 12. Trunk T — supplied information and the shape of joint inference

All T cards are mandatory.

### T01. Full conditional-information ledger

Compute exact entropy and conditional mutual information among:

- current goal;
- process route;
- expertise/mechanic;
- habit;
- persistent profile;
- communicative regime;
- artifact.

Report deterministic edges and zeros from the generative construction separately from measured

### T02. Complete supply-arm battery

For every latent X, compare recovery of every other latent Y with:

- no supply;
- true X supplied;
- shuffled X supplied;
- wrong but plausible X supplied;
- uncertain distribution over X supplied.

Do not hard-label uncertain upstream variables. Carry distributions forward.

### T03. Drives/profile upstream of process

This is G47/S-17. Supply the maker's true standing profile and measure improvement in process
recovery. Compare its magnitude, under one common scale, with supplying process to recover current
goal.

The result begins to distinguish a river-like drives → process → goal account from a more symmetric
joint constraint. It cannot by itself establish a biological processing order.

### T04. Mechanics/expertise as an entry point

This is G56. Supply the reader the maker's true transition or mechanic model and measure recovery
of current goal, process, and profile.

Cross:

- correct mechanic;
- related mechanic;
- wrong mechanic;
- generic mechanic with matched entropy;
- process trace supplied directly.

This is the missing test of whether expertise can unlock the rest as effectively as goal
legibility.

### T05. Entry-point and ceiling map

Repeat the supply battery across evidence difficulty. Determine whether apparent directionality
changes when one target is at ceiling or floor. Report shapes only from cells where both supplied
and unsupplied conditions can move.

### T06. Chain, triangle, river, or undirected factor graph

Compare candidate causal/inferential topologies by their predictions over the complete supply
matrix, not by metaphor. Use held-out interventions to score:

- directed chain;
- drives/process river;
- symmetric triangle;
- common-cause model;
- full joint factor graph.

If multiple topologies remain interventionally equivalent, report the equivalence class.

**T closure:** no topology claim survives if it is determined by the emitter's factorization alone
or by ceiling. The transferable result is the supply matrix; the topology interpretation remains
constructed-mechanism evidence.

## 13. Trunk D — director, distributed production, and causal reach

All D cards are mandatory.

### D01. Collaborative production ecology

Generate matched multi-agent artifacts under:

- one director controlling primary goal and cross-part allocation;
- a shared brief with independent local contributors;
- an editor controlling global order but not generation;
- a ratifier with final veto only;
- distributed agents coordinating through the shared artifact;
- independent contributors with no global controller.

Store full decision and message logs. Match final task quality, local choice counts, contributor
count, and source style.

### D02. Upstream causal-reach ruler

Intervene separately on:

- primary/director goal;
- one secondary goal;
- one implementation choice;
- final ratification;
- shared brief.

Measure how many and how strongly downstream choice distributions change. Report **causal reach**
separately from the number of decisions directly made.

### D03. Central director versus distributed shared brief

Hold the brief, local competence, and final coherence constant. Vary whether adaptation to
cross-part conflict comes from:

- a persistent central model;
- local rules plus shared brief;
- a final editor.

Artifact-only identification must beat coherence, style, and section-consistency baselines. Logs
provide the ceiling.

### D04. Contribution-level attribution

Ask who controlled:

- primary goal;
- section allocation;
- local wording or implementation;
- conflict resolution;
- final veto.

Token share is a baseline, not ground truth. Score each decision level and allow mixed
attribution.

### D05. Rewrite and sanding ladder

Pass outputs through increasingly strong local rewriting while preserving high-level structure.
Test which contributor signals survive:

- spelling/style normalization;
- sentence paraphrase;
- section rewrite;
- reordering;
- global structural rewrite.

Separate loss of surface authorship from preservation of upstream causal reach.

### D06. Prospective director prediction

Hide the next intervention: section attention, conflict resolution, secondary-goal sacrifice,
acceptance, or veto. Predict it from partial drafts and prior director behavior.

Retrospective coherence without prospective gain supports a story, not a director model.

### D07. Ratification versus failure to notice

Construct final artifacts that are identical under:

- deliberate inspection and acceptance;
- inspection and tolerance for another goal;
- failure to inspect;
- inability to inspect.

Confirm artifact-only non-identifiability where no downstream trace differs. Add later artifacts
in which ratification changes future policy and test when the histories become distinguishable.

**D closure:** if logs decisively identify control while artifacts remain at their calibrated
floor under D03/D06, close artifact-only director attribution for this ecology. Causal reach may
still be real and invisible.

## 16. Scoring and interpretation

### 16.1 Primary scores

Whenever a reader emits a distribution, use:

- multiclass log score or Brier score;
- top-1 accuracy for intuition;
- calibration error and reliability plot;
- risk versus coverage for abstention;
- posterior mass on the true equivalence class where one history is not identifiable.

For sequential inference report:

- low-evidence gain;
- evidence required to reach a frozen score;
- correction slope and half-life;
- asymptotic residual;
- self-directed error versus other errors.

For active selection report:

- expected and realized information gain;
- final prediction improvement;
- policy regret against the exact optimum;
- evidence cost;
- unnecessary and missed probes.

For uptake report:

- downstream task regret;
- argmax preservation;
- wrong-direction policy movement;
- process, belief, preference, and imitation changes separately;
- reversibility after reliable correction.

For hierarchy report:

- direct event count;
- causal reach under intervention;
- artifact-only attribution;
- prospective next-intervention score;
- records-aware ceiling.

### 16.2 Effect size and known-answer scaling

Do not use “interval excludes zero” as sufficient evidence. Report:

- overlap with the strongest null;
- direction relative to null;
- effect as a fraction of the live known-positive or oracle gap;
- per-world distribution;
- architecture survival surface;
- full factorial matrix.

Absolute acceptance bars should be derived before the full run from:

- exact chance or identity where available;
- a fixed fraction of the known-positive separation;
- the smallest effect that would alter pursuit;
- a pilot used only for power and then excluded from confirmation.

### 16.3 Multiplicity

Register every estimator, factor, transform, distance, and abandoned arm. Discovery may use false
discovery-rate control for ranking, but no discovery p-value becomes a confirmation claim.

Allocate confirmation worlds and makers before discovery scoring. Freeze:

- construction;
- reader path;
- distance;
- evidence doses;
- score;
- exclusions;
- direction;
- criterion.

Only then inspect confirmation.

## 19. Promotion, closure, and curator interruption

### 19.1 Flight candidate: efficient self-to-other inversion

Interrupt the curator only if all hold:

- self-first beats the information- and compute-matched generic route selectively for near makers;
- the effect survives policy/source separation and prior permutation;
- diagnostic conflict produces correction rather than persistent false consensus;
- the route predicts a hidden continuation or chooses a better probe;
- the shape repeats across independent worlds and a fresh domain;
- exact and PyMDP results agree in direction or their difference is itself explained.

This is a constructed engineering flight and a confirmation trigger, not human empathy evidence.

### 19.2 Flight candidate: calibrated selective uptake

All must hold:

- maker reconstruction is calibrated;
- source reliability and maker competence are separated;
- process uptake improves while indiscriminate belief or value uptake does not;
- wrong/confident posteriors are gated better than MAP or unconditional copying;
- reliable counterevidence reverses harmful movement;
- a fresh-domain policy benefits.

### 19.3 Flight candidate: readable hierarchical hand

All must hold:

- an upstream intervention has broader causal reach than matched local edits;
- artifact evidence distinguishes central adaptation from shared-brief coherence;
- the inferred controller predicts a held-out intervention;
- rewrite and role reassignment do not reduce the result to source style;
- mixed attribution remains calibrated.

### 19.4 Strong closures

- **Self route closed:** S04–S07 show no selective benefit beyond matched generic inference and
  projection is not corrected.
- **Active probing closed:** Q never beats uncertainty sampling or recovers its extra cost.
- **Communicative assumption bounded:** B helps only because an unmatched surface cue survives, or
  regime is artifact-underdetermined after matching.
- **Uptake mechanism closed:** downstream action moves only when preferences are directly inserted,
  irrespective of reconstruction quality.
- **Value residue closed:** R estimators fit identity/history but fail cross-context prediction.
- **Topology underdetermined:** D/F logs remain decisive while artifacts sit at calibrated floor.
- **Supply topology unresolved:** T candidate graphs remain interventionally equivalent.

### 19.5 Early interruption conditions

Otherwise run without daily curator interaction. Interrupt only for:

- a flight conjunction;
- an ontology change required to continue;
- a need for paid access, cloud compute, human recruitment, or external contact;
- destructive or repository-wide migration;
- a result proving a currently used ruler invalid in a way that changes prior published claims.

## 23. Pre-mortem

V12 will produce a persuasive but worthless result if any of these occur:

1. **Similarity is truth leakage.** The self route receives a closer prior without an
   information-matched control.
2. **Self is only a prompt.** The condition adds tokens or deliberation but never uses a measured
   self-policy.
3. **Maker identity substitutes for maker policy.** Style or source labels predict the answer.
4. **The emitter writes the conclusion.** Cooperative, layered, or value worlds differ through
   unmatched cheap features.
5. **Projection is called empathy.** Confidence rises for similar-looking makers while conflict
   correction fails.
6. **Inference is called uptake.** Accurate reconstruction is treated as caring, cooperation, or
   preference change.
7. **Uptake is direct injection.** The true maker preference is placed into C_AIF without a
   calibrated posterior and then “recovered” downstream.
8. **PyMDP approximation becomes ontology.** A mean-field error is interpreted as a property of
   maker inference.
9. **Artifacts are treated as more informative than they are.** Identical final artifacts are
   assigned different histories without records.
10. **Artifact rows become independent makers.** Huge n disguises one constructed lineage.
11. **The forest becomes a garden of forking paths.** Every distance, estimator, and discarded arm
    is not registered.
12. **The coding agent downsizes the program.** A few roots run and the remaining cards become an
    optional appendix.
13. **Duration replaces information.** Compute is spent duplicating seeds after a construction is
    dead.
14. **CPU work starves the GPU program.** Worker or BLAS oversubscription reduces Sounding Line
    throughput even though V12 uses no VRAM.
15. **A constructed analogue is reported as human evidence.** No result in this file crosses that
    boundary.

The gates, manifest, and closure rules exist to make these failures visible before prose is
written.
