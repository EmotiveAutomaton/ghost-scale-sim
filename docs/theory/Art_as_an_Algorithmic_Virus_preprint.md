## I. INTRODUCTION

To align artificial intelligence with human aesthetics, we must first strip art of its mysticism and reduce it to measurable structural telemetry: art iscompressed intent. Under this biological model, emotional resonance and structural emergence are not accidents; they are the successful somatic decoding of a creator’s nested, subconscious priors [1]. Therefore,art is simply any artifact into which a cognitive agent concentrates a massive density of thermodynamic resources and decisionmaking to serve a goal. Artifacts with more decisions are more artful. Domain is irrelevant: a painting, a bridge, a navigational route, and a smile all execute the exact same biological process and are judged as more or less artful depending on the concentration of decisions made in their creation. This definition accommodates apparent counterexamples from art history. Duchamp’s Fountain(1917) was a massproduced urinal submitted as art, and involved minimal physical fabrication but concentrated enormous decision-density into a single act of selection, contextualization, and institutional provocation [2]. Under the present framework, the artfulness resides not in the manufactured object but in the hierarchical compression of conceptual decisions that Duchamp’s act encoded. Similarly, aleatory composition and process-based art deliberately remove low-level decisions, but the meta-decision to constrain or relinquish control itself carries high intentional density. The framework thus distinguishes between the absence of decisions (low artfulness) and the deliberate, structured removal of decisions (potentially high artfulness), a distinction that maps naturally onto the hierarchical bundling described by automaticity. I will defineappreciationas: identifying the actions used to create an artifact, using that information to derive the goals of the creator via Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) [3], and using the alignment of those inferred goals with the viewer’s own values to determine the magnitude of the resulting synaptic update. Within this biological framework, the observer’s intent-extraction protocol is modeled at the computational level as Maximum Entropy Inverse Reinforcement Learning (Max Ent IRL) [4], assuming the creator maximized reward while maintaining maximum behavioral entropy. In other words, appreciation is the act of inferring a maker’s intent, learning to mimic the (expectedly) efficient steps that maker took to achieve that intent, and then halting the process if the inferred intent diverges from the viewer’s. We are not passive recipients of artistic inputs. We actively engage with the world through the lens of intentionality [5]. The Theory of Mind network we use for modeling the perspectives of others is notably active when we engage with something we are told is “art” [6], [7]. Behavioral evidence directly supports this prediction. Messingschlager and Appel (2025) demonstrated that labeling an artifact as AI-generated reduces appreciation, and crucially that this effect is mediated by the perceived agency and experience ascribed to the creator (both Theory of Mind constructs) [8]. Their finding that mind perception statistically mediates the appreciation gap is consistent with the present framework’s prediction that the devaluation of generative art is driven by a failure to converge upon a coherent latent goal rather than by surface-level aesthetic judgment; the neural mechanism of this deactivation is tested directly in H1. To address this, I will propose the Ghost Scale, a UX framework that acts as a cognitive affordance for the viewer in the identification of intentionality. This scale, properly deployed, would also be effective in both the immediate avoidance of model collapse [9] and eventual AI alignment (see H5). Cultural transmission of values is already optimized for the biological execution of inverse reinforcement learning through the intent extraction of top-down precision weighting [10]. Currently, we train AI by extracting value information from bottom-up aesthetic capture mechanisms using reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) [11]. This will inevitably result in the creation of shallow output in terms of both objective artistic assessment and the hierarchical alignment with human values. We should instead take advantage of this widespread human behavior and capability by utilizing existing neurological and cultural architecture to capture appropriate value information through Cooperative Inverse Reinforcement Learning (CIRL) [12] and subsequently using it to inform convergence of world models such as GENIE and Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures [13]. Human brains optimize to extract intention from their environment already. Addressing alignment requires not just coding better guardrails, but learning to capture humanity’s constant broadcasting of value and intent information.

## II. THE NEUROBIOLOGY OF INTENT EXTRACTION

Analysis begins with physical artifacts. To process the language of intent, the viewer’s neocortex constructs a possible physical and cognitive trajectory for the creation of an object through the process ofembodied simulation[14]. Mirror neurons are hypothesized to contribute bottom-up error correction for the top-down theory of mind network [15], which together map inferred behaviors and their physical deviations onto the viewer’s own viscero-motor and somatosensory architecture. Under the Thousand Brains Theory[16], this parsing is distributed across tens of thousands of independent cortical columns. Deep visual processing of an artifact necessitates that these columns functionally simulate the kinetic physics required for execution. This spatial mapping extends to abstract, higher-order concepts. Evidence from fMRI studies on the entorhinal cortex demonstrates that humans organize abstract conceptual knowledge using a grid-like code, effectively mapping non-spatial cognitive trajectories onto geometric coordinate systems [17]. Therefore, when observing a programmer manipulate data architectures, the observer’s embodied simulation still fires; the Theory of Mind network maps these abstract problem-solving strategies onto conceptual reference frames, error-correcting the inferred latent goal against the observer’s own spatial and cognitive geometry. However, another source of error correction for the simulation of higher order concepts is the mirror neuron system simulating internal states (i.e.,affective empathy) [18]. Partially through an equivalent error correction through conceptual grid space and partially through the use of lower-order data from the non-grid architecture as a precision weighting mechanism for cortical prediction errors, bottom-up processing gives error correction through empathy in the simulation of the creation of the artifact. Through both top-down processing viacognitive empathyin the Theory of Mind network and bottom-up processing via affective empathy in the limbic system and Mirror Neuron System, we work to minimize free energy [19] and converge upon a cohesive model that explains the creation of that artifact.

### A. The Simulation Firewall of Disgust

The Default Mode Networkoperates as a primary framework for simulation and is a core integration hub for the Theory of Mind [20]. While simulating, the amount of update of neural weights is determined by the plasticity gating mechanism governed by the Salience Network[21]. This network, when active, encourages the update of neural weights during simulation. Epistemic disgust moderates this process, triggered by a large value misalignment in the converged model. The calculation of the value disagreement itself implies simulation, which inherently drives Hebbian learning due to gating imperfections [22]. This forced Hebbian learning is likely the mechanism for indoctrination and propaganda, and the gating mechanism of disgust that resists these effects will be reflected in the first term of theintent extraction limit.

## III. THE INTENT EXTRACTION LIMIT

I formally define artistic appreciation (Ψ) as the magnitude of systemic prediction error reduction achieved via the synaptic internalization of a conspecific’s behavioral heuristics. This integration operates under the Free Energy Principle [19], where the observer executes what is formally equivalent to Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) to reverse-engineer a hidden reward function. This equivalence is not stipulated as analogy but derived from modeling both agents under the FEP (see Appendix A.1). The process is governed by metabolic constraints and value alignment. The magnitude of this synaptic update is driven by Bayesian surprise, quantified as the Kullback-Leibler divergence [23] between the observer’s prior expectation of human motivation [P(R)] and the updated posterior extracted from the physical artifact [P(R|τ)]. Because biological neural networks chunk information to manage working memory constraints, this integration is bounded by the algorithmic cognitive load. Using Variational Inference (VI), this friction is expressed as the minimization of the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the observer’s biological prior (a flat entropy distribution representing baseline metabolic constraints) and the updated posterior extracted from the artifact. Integration is catalyzed by epistemic trust (κ), evaluated under Zahavian signaling constraints based on unforgeable production costs [24]. The logarithmic amplifier−ln(1−κ) was selected for three converging reasons. First, it captures the empirically observed nonlinear scaling of interpersonal and automation trust, where incremental evidence near certainty produces disproportionate behavioral commitment [25]. Second, it enforces the biologically necessary constraint that κcan never reach 1: trust is asymptotic, never absolute, and the logarithmic barrier prevents the integration multiplier from diverging to infinity. Third,−ln(1−κ)is the informationtheoretic self-information of untrustworthiness, scaling the gate in units commensurate with the KL divergence term it modulates. If this asymptotic bound is artificially bypassed, the brain is forced to hallucinate intent to resolve the resulting computational paralysis (see Appendix C, H6). The brain’s active inference mechanisms operate under a hard metabolic constraint [19], [26], modeled here as an autonomic sigmoidal gating function that aborts inference when expected information gain falls below a metabolic threshold. The sigmoidal form is standard in computational neuroscience for modeling neural population threshold responses, where a continuous input is converted to a binary activation decision via accumulated evidence [27]. The gain parameterkreflects the meta-precision of the gating function itself (the precision with which the brain weighs sensory precision against the disgust threshold) [27]. This gate balances bottom-upsensory precision weighting(ω) against a dynamic threshold of epistemic disgust (θ E,C ). This threshold scales inversely with the organism’s available metabolic reserves (E) and directly tracks the alignment of the artifact with the observer’s survival priors (C), functioning as the foundational reward against which the creator’s inferred state (R c) is measured: θE,C (κ) =θ base(E) +λD KL (Q(R|τ)∥P c(R)) The complete formalization of the Intent Extraction Limit is therefore expressed as: Ψ =  1 1 +e −k(ω−θE,C (κ))  [−ln(1−κ)] ×D KL (Q(R|τ)∥P 0(R)) Computationally, within latent diffusion architectures, the causal link between intent and physical execution is severed, corresponding to the collapse of idiosyncratic epsilonprediction errors. Simultaneously, the epistemic disgust thresholdθ E,C (κ)acts as an autonomic regularization penalty preventing weight updates from misaligned latent structures. This framework constrains the mechanics of memetic transmission to two distinct biological failure modes:

### A. The Epistemic Disgust Firewall(Ψ→0):

If the creator’s inferred goal radically diverges from the observer’s fundamental values (P c(R)), the ideological divergence termD KL (Q(R|τ)∥P c(R))spikes. This drives the disgust thresholdθ E,C to mathematically override the trust variableκ. The sigmoid evaluates to near zero, closing the gate. The brain may recognize the aesthetic complexity of the artifact, but it autonomically blocks Hebbian integration to protect its own network weights from a misaligned memetic signature.

### B. The Generative Crash(L(R)→ ∞):

When processing synthetic media generated by latent diffusion architectures [28], idiosyncratic deviations mathematically collapse because structural execution is decoupled from a biological motor-policy. While the artifact possesses high Shannon entropy (visual complexity) [29], it lacks the high Kolmogorov complexity (description length) inherent to biological constraint satisfaction [30], [31]. Because the variance is stochastic rather than intentional, the biological priorP 0(R) cannot map the output to a coherent human hidden state, and the algorithmic cognitive load required to model this unconstrained space diverges toward infinity(L(R)→ ∞). The biological Max Ent IRL calculation terminates, defaulting the extracted reward to zero(R inf erred= 0)to conserve finite computational resources.

## IV. AI ALIGNMENT IMPLICATIONS

The preceding biological mechanisms suggest (but do not formally entail) specific implications for computational alignment. The following structural correspondences are proposed as extensions of the framework, contingent on assumptions about the scalability of CIRL architectures and the capacity of world models to capture hierarchical intent. The biological Max Ent IRL executed during appreciation is derived from the Free Energy Principle directly (see Appendix A.1) and maps to the inverse of next-token prediction in language models. Embodied simulation maps functionally to the forward passes of world models. The second correspondence is treated as a computational-level analogy; the first is a derived consequence of the shared theoretical substrate. AI alignment will require extracting value data, using it to model humanity’s higher-order values, and then turning that resulting value information into appropriately aligned action. I will address only the first two issues here, starting with the second.

### A. ModelingR

The prevailing methodology of reinforcement learning is optimizing for low-level sensory data. RLHF optimizes a single scalar bound to cognitively fatigued raters prioritizing surface heuristics (e.g., formatting, tone, superficial accuracy) over alignment with higher-order latent values (e.g., helpfulness, honesty, compassion) [32]. Unfortunately, interacting with automated output turns off our theory of mind network and higher-order reasoning. By definition, we are not evaluating, rating, or providing any input on the axis of what humanity truly seems to value: the minimization ofD KL (Q(R|τ)∥P 0(R))(i.e., evidence of efficient, effective, and novel decisionmaking; orartfulness). Instead, we optimize foraesthetics: the combination of base heuristics of attractiveness and the initial impressions of depth (κ)before the Intent Extraction Limit converges. Next-token prediction and latent diffusion are explicitly mapping what can be observed:P(τ t+1|τ1:t). We are not even capable of identifying the latent cause(R), but the information is being put out into the world constantly. Architectures such as Google’s GENIE [33] and Meta’s Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures [13] work to decode the higher-order reasoning that would be necessary to isolate and align with humanity’sR. However, world models of all kinds are important explorations into this space. I argue that these architectures are fundamentally required for identification and alignment with humanity’s latent(R).

### B. Extracting Value Data

However, possessing the architecture to model does not solve the sycophancy loop of RLHF [34]. To align the machine, we must utilize Cooperative Inverse Reinforcement Learning[12]. Within a CIRL topology, the interaction is framed as a cooperative game where the human reward function remains explicitly hidden from the artificial agent. This mathematical constraint ensures the system operates under permanent epistemic uncertainty, forcing it to utilize observed behavioral trajectories and cultural artifacts(τ)as evidentiary telemetry to update its probability distributions of human intent, mimicking the biological transmission of values. When using this method, we are recreating humanity’s native language for the appreciation of art via inverse reinforcement learning and actively using it to extract value data that humanity is not just passively emitting but actively signaling. This opens the door for a potential step towards a solution: one that reduces the environmental epistemic uncertaintyκ while helping humans with the cognitive load of calculating a need for epistemic disgust and generative crashes. To solve both problems, we need a cultural meta-signal for degrees of AI-generated content. We need a cognitive affordance for both human minds and AI training. Humans may conserve energy with such a signal by quickly triggering a generative crash and climbing into higher-order reasoning (such as guessing a prompt’s motivation). This would allow the viewer to save energy calculatingθ E,C by quickly feeding the user the answer of a low-valueκ. The epistemic uncertainty inherent in CIRL may also be reduced through the same method. The design requirements for the affordance are that it be unobtrusive-yet-visible, intuitively learned, exist in a largely uncontested design space, be self-enforcing by design, robust against future technological development, and simple to apply. Most of all, it must be appealing to artists (defined as “having depth of reasoning behind the decision process”).

## V. THE GHOST SCALE

In that same artifact, the main background color is the color of output that is devoid of higher-order reasoning. ## VI. EMPIRICAL PREDICTIONS FOR FUTURE WORK

To anchor this theoretical framework in rigorous scientific utility, I propose the following six empirical predictions. While this section outlines the conceptual mechanisms and failure states, the formal experimental paradigms, neurophysiological metrics, and psychometric formalizations required for peer replication are detailed thoroughly in Appendix C.

### A. The Generative Crash

H1: Pupillometric dilation and EEG markers of sustained cognitive engagement [36] will demonstrate a measurable autonomic drop within 2–4 seconds of a subject being informed that a viewed artifact is AI-generated, compared to identical artifacts labeled as human-made, reflecting the rapid deactivation of active inference processing rather than a slower aesthetic judgment. This temporally differentiates a cognitive artistic/aesthetic judgment from an instantaneous autonomic metabolic shutoff. Existing behavioral evidence confirms that AI attribution reduces aesthetic appreciation via ToM-mediated mechanisms [8]; H1 tests whether this behavioral signal has an identifiable neural substrate in the autonomic precision-weighting system. Competing account:An alternative explanation holds that the AI label triggers a social categorization response (”this isn’t worth my time”) rather than a metabolic shutoff. These accounts make distinguishable temporal predictions. A social status reassessment should produce a gradual, evaluative decline in engagement as the observer consciously reassesses the artifact’s cultural value. The present framework predicts a discontinuous step function: an abrupt autonomic collapse in precision weighting occurring faster than conscious aesthetic judgment can execute. The three-phase design isolates this distinction; the temporal signature of Phase 3 discriminates between the two accounts. Failure:If AI art sustains prolonged cognitive load without causing an autonomic attention drop, or if the post-reveal decline follows a gradual evaluative trajectory indistinguishable from conscious reassessment, the computational overload theory is false.

### B. Inverse Reinforcement Learning Failure

H2: When tasked with explicitly reverse-engineering the goal of an artwork, subject response variance will be significantly higher, and inter-rater reliability significantly lower, for AI-generated artifacts than for human-generated artifacts. Because generative media lacks a true latent reward function (R= 0), the observer’s forced Variational Inference calculation lacks physical or semantic anchors. Subjects will individually execute a VI over-fit, hallucinating highly idiosyncratic local minima(R hallucinated). Thematic variance across raters will approach maximum entropy (statistical noise), whereas human artifacts will yield clustered thematic variance converging on the true, extractableR. This maximum variance serves as psychometric proof that the artifact is acting as stochastic noise within the biological system. Participants who fail to identify AI art as such will fail to converge upon a sufficiently accurate description of the latent reward functionR, instead detailing their own forced VI overfit.

### C. Ghost Scale Validation

H3: After training in the scale, reading comprehension fatigue will demonstrate a measurable, dose-dependent relationship with the ghost scale. Reading text at 60% opacity will result in a measurable metabolic trade-off: increased localized oculomotor strain (due to the perceptual tax of lower contrast) coupled with significantly lower systemic cognitive fatigue (as the brain autonomically aborts the DMN’s massive Active Inference calculations).

### D. Biomechanics

H4: Artists tasked with replicating complex AI-generated geometry will demonstrate a measurable loss in their own biomechanical fluidity (tracked via stylus accelerometry) in subsequent free-draw tasks, compared to a control group replicating human-made art. The brain attempts to execute an uncomputable physical action space, temporarily degrading its own kinetic reference frames. Directly using cortical reference frames without higherorder structure as guidance for physical tasks should result in actively corrupted biological motor models.

### E. Alignment Convergence

H5: Reward models trained on high-decision-density human artifacts will show equivalent performance on standard language benchmarks but measurably lower sycophancy rates and higher preference generalization than reward models trained on quality-matched AI-generated corpora. Separating surface-level quality from high-level intentdensity is a relatively novel concept. Note that reducing sycophancy is a primary indicator of a successful CIRL. If confirmed, this result reframes the Ghost Scale not merely as a cultural affordance but as a necessary data-preprocessing tool for alignment research: a method for filtering training corpora by intent-density rather than surface quality, capturing a variable that current automated data-cleaning pipelines cannot isolate. Failure: If reward model performance is statistically equivalent across corpora matched for quality but not intent-density, decision-density is not a meaningful alignment training variable. F . A Trust Exploit H6: If a subject is placed under clinical hypnosis to decouple the DMN [37], the biological firewall of epistemic disgust will be bypassed. The subject’s brain will process known AIgenerated art with the same sustained neural activation as human art, writing the unresisted data directly to predictive models. Longitudinal exposure under this bypass predicts the biological integration and reproduction of synthetic heuristics (e.g., algorithmic style markers) in subsequent human behavioral outputs.

## VII. CONCLUSION

We have successfully engineered silicon to execute the autonomic, fast-thinking reflexes of human automaticity at global scale. True alignment, however, demands that we computationally scale our deepest cognitive adaptation: the architecture of intention-reading driven by empathy. The framework presented here formalizes this architecture as biological Max Ent IRL, identifies its failure mode under generative synthesis, proposes a cognitive affordance to mitigate that failure, and suggests that alignment research pivot from surface-level reward hacking toward the cooperative extraction of humanity’s latent reward function. We must cease building models that only predict our physical outputs, and instead build architectures that reverseengineer our hidden biological reward functions, grounding human continuity in the rigorous, algorithmic enforcement of systemic empathy and an accompanying kindness.
