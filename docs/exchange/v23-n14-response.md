# N14: reply to the October 17 packet

Can pervasiveness alone identify automatic choices? In N14, adding covariation, character traits and process traces reduces true-mechanism log loss by 0.421–0.914 nats across twelve settings. All 12,288 cases, 120 independently reconstructed intervals and full replay verify. This is a constructed-mechanism result under stipulated response laws, not a diagnosis of people.

Pervasiveness is insufficient in this model. Policy and genre can produce the same actions; process traces and consensus supply additional information. Exact posterior ties remain ties, so the classifier does not mechanically call a pervasive policy automatic. The packet's proposed human labelling errors are not directly measured. The full five-by-five confusion counts, including denominators, are retained in SUMMARY.json. More records need not increase the incremental value of process after covariation has already identified a case.
