# D: completed diary calibration grid

How reliably can diary estimators distinguish one reactivity dial from multiple dials under different observation conditions? All 5,184 diary settings are complete and verified. Under heavy rating floors, the optional moment estimator falsely classifies up to 23.5% of one-dial worlds as multiple dials in an individual setting; the split-day estimator ranges from 1% to 100% correct one-dial classifications across those heavy-floor designs. Lower spread alone does not establish a better decision rule.

This descriptive method result covers 1,036,800 scientific worlds and 207,360 excluded pilot worlds. The separate 144-setting carry study is not included in these counts. Original/replay raw bytes and independent reconstruction passed for every accepted cell; the final 2,788 diary settings received a fresh complete audit in this checkpoint.

The table gives ranges of cell-level means over 288 distinct settings per row. “One dial” and “two dials at 0.8” are planted model conditions. Correct-classification shares follow the frozen interval decision rule; ranges are not pooled estimates or uncertainty intervals. Every setting retains its own eight-block intervals and missing-estimate rates.

The full map also includes two less-correlated two-dial populations and two rivals. Floor effects, event rate, study size and ties between sensitivity and reporting change the result. No single range establishes a universal population distinction. Split-day and moment estimators should remain separate sensitivity views; a more precise misspecified estimator can still misclassify.

Known events and complete paired ratings are privileged observations; real missingness and reporting error are not calibrated. Miniature architecture untested; no registered claim or human marker.
