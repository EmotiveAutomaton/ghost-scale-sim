# D: reaction and carry in a diary

Can estimated diary reactivity and carry appear correlated when the planted parameters are independent? The complete 144-cell constructed diary map quantifies that correlation and the recovery of each parameter; it is a method calibration, not evidence about people.

The table shows the mean estimated reactivity–carry correlation, with its 95% eight-block interval, for 2,272 people and 240 prompts. Rows are the three V30 parameter centres; columns are prompt gaps in minutes. The SHOW view uses binary shows.

| Centre | 40 minutes | 90 minutes | 150 minutes | 240 minutes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| EXPOSED | 0.282 (0.278–0.285) | 0.201 (0.197–0.204) | 0.109 (0.106–0.111) | 0.033 (0.030–0.036) |
| REACTIVE | 0.269 (0.267–0.271) | 0.279 (0.275–0.282) | 0.206 (0.203–0.208) | 0.070 (0.067–0.074) |
| STICKY | 0.293 (0.288–0.297) | 0.286 (0.281–0.291) | 0.251 (0.249–0.253) | 0.190 (0.188–0.192) |

The full grid also covers 300 and 1,200 people, 60 prompts and a noisy continuous intensity view. Aggregates retain true parameter correlations, recovery and available-person denominators. Reactivity and clearing are generated independently; finite-sample realized correlations vary.

These are separate descriptive slope fits, not a mechanistic inverse fit. The grid does not cover ES3’s over-240-minute contrast. Sounding Line ES3 can use these estimates to calibrate apparent associations; ToMpathy receives this as a constructed-method boundary. The miniature architecture is untested.
