# Public-request calibration: independently verified

We tested whether pooling public task requests hides opposing errors in local-goal probabilities. At 2,048 labels, request pooling hides up to 0.11078 percentage points of absolute calibration discrepancy in the predictive bank under native weighting. Independent reconstruction, all 2,196 paired estimates and both complete replays verify this constructed-method diagnostic. No practical-impact threshold was specified; historical goal correspondence and human intent remain unestablished.

The frozen diagnostic retains both legal public task requests, sixteen development
laws, two training draws, five fit seeds, four label budgets, four readouts, two
forecasts, three positions and three local goals. Grouping uses the observed
requested purpose, never the maker's hidden adopted purpose. Changing the artifact
or operations while retaining the request cannot change group membership.
Native weighting preserves each generator's public-frame distribution; equal-frame
weighting gives all 704 visible frames equal mass. No new fit, sample, model setting
or protected lineage is used.

The largest displayed native-weighted cancellation is the first position's
presentation goal. The first and third positions' dependency goals show none
beyond rounding. This diagnoses errors hidden by aggregation; it changes no
forecast and establishes no practical benefit. No practical-impact threshold was
specified. Intervals at roundoff scale are not evidence of an effect. Narrow
equal-frame law intervals can reflect shared forecasts and frame weights;
they do not measure all training uncertainty. Separate draw and seed means are
retained without treating them as independent worlds.
