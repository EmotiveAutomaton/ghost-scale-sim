# Partial disclosure before storage - 25 September 2026

We tested whether partial disclosure of the source prior improves retained coverage. It improves expected retained mass in 348 of 1,260 distinct partial-disclosure problems, capturing the full-disclosure benefit in 342. Independent rational reconstruction verifies all 57,344 roster rows, their 4,300,800 disclosure evaluations and both complete replays. This is a constructed-method result about gross information value in a finite storage library; it does not establish forecast accuracy or process correspondence.

Coverage is the probability mass of source records retained by a storage mask.
The experiment enumerates all five partitions of three supplied source-prior
identities and all 15 mixtures with weights in quarters. A disclosed partition
cell permits a conditional storage choice within the same finite byte budget.
The endpoints reveal nothing or the complete prior identity; the three intermediate
partitions each distinguish one prior from the other two. Information is free.

The 348 improvements are among 1,260 distinct intermediate-partition problems.
Of these improvements, 342 attain the full-disclosure value and six leave a gap.
Repeated structural answers across source rosters are not independent replications.
Zero-probability cells have undefined conditional probabilities and retained mass,
zero contribution, and every mask tied. The frozen largest-mask tie choice is
retained even in those cells; byte feasibility holds for every possible mask.

Independent reconstruction verifies seven structural cost records, 28,672 source
