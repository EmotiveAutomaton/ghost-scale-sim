# Retrospective reports at retained reachable posteriors: prepared alternative

Does grouping hypotheses by their remaining state schedules preserve the effect
of a newly supplied report about a past maker state at posteriors actually reached
by the accepted filter? This B-family alternative is independent of adversarial
full-simplex witnesses and of joint-forecast partitions. Its handler is implemented;
isolated controls and complete synthetic timing must pass before admission. It
has no admitted outcome and does not repeat the completed future-forecast test.

Use every retained complete posterior from the accepted original and source-omitted
unknown-time/type rosters, both observation draws, all eight development laws,
maker states, change conditions and source conditions. Use the same available
checkpoints as the accepted future-schedule quotient: 8/16/17/20/32 at horizon 32,
16/32 at horizon 128. Mark absent checkpoints unavailable. Do not regenerate
observations or add fits, seeds or protected lineages.

For every earlier time, context and possible endpoint, define a separately sourced
retrospective emission under that time's maker state. Its likelihood is the native
endpoint law. Compare exact full-hypothesis conditioning with a quotient-only
update whose within-group likelihood uses the original prior conditional weights,
frozen before report outcomes. Also include the exact current posterior's
within-group conditional likelihood as an explicitly privileged identity control.
Enumerate every report; weight forecasts by their reference predictive report
probabilities, retaining zero-probability reports as impossible observations.
Do not interpret a stochastic retrospective emission as a second independent
copy of the original observed action. A report explicitly copied from an already
known source must produce the existing posterior unchanged; test that separately.

Retain complete group memberships, conditional likelihoods, initial and updated
quotient weights, and all remaining-time/four-context endpoint forecasts. Report
update and forecast errors separately, with paired law summaries conditional on
the retained trajectories. A prior-weight quotient is a declared bounded rival,
not every possible update using additional state. This tests reachable cases,
not a universal representational theorem or historical process correspondence.

Known answers must include stationary identical full schedules, constant emission
laws, supplied exact conditional likelihoods, same-source duplicate identity,
impossible reports and deliberate wrong group mapping. Freeze lossless complete
enumeration, uncertainty, source and a full-size synthetic cost model before
dispatch. The initial inclusive cap is 3,600 CPU seconds within the existing B
family and global ceilings, including tests, failures, replay and review. A
tractability failure is scoped; no sampled replacement or automatic cap extension.

Before execution, freeze batches of 32 posterior rows solely as a memory limit.
All 256 paired rows in each available law/source/checkpoint cell participate.
The original prior assigns half its mass to stationary hypotheses and half to
all changed hypotheses. The rival uses that prior's conditional law inside each
future group. The posterior-conditional identity control uses the current full
posterior and therefore has additional input privilege.

Enumerate all report times through the checkpoint, four contexts and eight
endpoints. Each time/context receives equal descriptive weight; possible endpoints
receive their exact predictive probability. Preserve impossible reports separately.
Report total variation between updated group distributions (half the sum of
absolute probability differences), the largest future coordinate discrepancy,
and mean future endpoint total variation. Average paired observation draws inside
each law before law-level uncertainty; do not bootstrap individual reports.

Retain full posterior weights, original group maps and schedules, fixed prior
conditional likelihoods, all mixed-group exact report numerators, both report
probabilities and each report's diagnostics. Singleton-group numerators are the
retained singleton weight times its supplied likelihood. Together these are a
lossless factorization of every exact/rival updated group weight and every future
forecast; no probability cutoff or report sampling is permitted. Fixture tests
must expand all factors and every future coordinate against direct Bayes. Timing
is separate from deterministic evidence. Independent reconstruction and paired
regroup remain required in addition to both complete replays.
