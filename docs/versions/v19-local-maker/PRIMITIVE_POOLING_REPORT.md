# Primitive input pooling: independently verified

We tested whether pooling equivalent tool inputs improves a frozen forward model. Before new feedback, correct pooling improves original-tool forecast loss by 0.05570–0.06116 nats but worsens changed-tool loss by 0.08843–0.08948 nats. Independent reconstruction, all 9,120 paired estimates and both complete replays verify this constructed-method reversal. Supplied equivalence does not establish learned mechanics, historical process correspondence or human intent.

Logarithmic forecast loss is prediction error measured in nats; lower is better.
The first table reports pooled-minus-unpooled loss before new feedback, with
95% paired coefficient-lineage intervals. Rows cross both retained training-support
conditions and the correct or deliberately wrong map. Columns distinguish the
original and changed tool. Native query probabilities weight each of eight
lineages; both saved training draws are averaged within each lineage.

Correct pooling exceeds the declared 0.02-nat practical margin in opposite
directions under the two tools. The wrong grouping worsens both. Correct input
equivalence holds for both tools, but pooling retains old empirical outcomes;
its correctness does not guarantee transfer of the learned transition law.
The operation may ignore an undo buffer under either rule while producing a
different endpoint after the rule changes. The result separates representation
validity from accuracy of retained empirical dynamics.

Sixteen truthful reports do not remove that reversal. Across both support modes
and input orders, correct pooling raises changed-tool loss by 0.08727–0.08855
nats relative to matching unpooled updates. Within the correctly pooled arm,
feedback improves overall changed-tool loss only 0.00268–0.00281 nats, below the
practical margin throughout its conditional intervals. Against unpooled updates,
queries whose endpoints change lose 0.34687–0.35009 nats; unchanged endpoints
improve 0.00768–0.00824 nats. All changed/stay and original-support strata,
including wrong-feedback and reverse-order contrasts, remain in the full record.

**Validation:** all 433 deterministic outputs agree across original, adjacent and
extracted-source executions. Original runs bind 515 source files and 33 inputs;
the independent checker binds 519 sources and 436 inputs. All source archives,
plan/environment identities, execution measurements and exported members verify.
The checker independently rebuilds scalar counts, tables, explicit path-sum
forecasts, proper scores, native masses from 221,184 retained paths, support
strata, group denominators and ten anonymous reader packets. Its largest absolute
discrepancy is 3.11e-15, below 1e-12. Parent training and native path-probability
validation inherit separately bound reviews; the checker does not refit them.
Eight internal, ten original isolated and ten isolated checker controls pass.
Corruption controls exercise counts, tables, forecasts, scores, masses, maps,
group denominators, reader fields and stratum coverage. The broader 61-file
regression separately retains 247 Ghost and five Torch passes, zero skips.

**Warrant:** exploratory constructed-method advantage under the original tool and
reversal under changed mechanics; miniature — architecture untested. Conditional
intervals quantify eight coefficient lineages given the retained fits and fixed
feedback sets, not universal training or evidence uncertainty. No causal-access,
historical process-correspondence, human-intent or confirmatory claim follows.
