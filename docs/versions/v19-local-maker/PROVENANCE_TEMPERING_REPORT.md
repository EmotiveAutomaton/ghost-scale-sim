# Fixed likelihood tempering: wider sets, mixed maker-state effects

Can reducing observation weight repair uncertainty distorted by undisclosed copies? Lower fixed powers widen current-maker candidate sets and increase their observed inclusion, while maker-state loss improves for stationary makers and can worsen after a change. All final copied-stream endpoint-loss intervals for lower powers versus one stay within the declared 0.02-nat margin. Independent reconstruction, all 7,920 estimates and both complete replays verify this constructed-method tradeoff under supplied laws; it establishes neither calibrated uncertainty, inferred provenance, historical process correspondence nor human intent.

The fixed comparison reuses eight coefficient lineages, two observation draws,
sixteen equally weighted makers, lengths 32/128 and every original checkpoint.
The two evidence presentations are identity-aware and identity-omitted versions
of 4,096 saved streams. The 8,192 stream evaluations are not new observations.
Powers 1, 0.75 and 0.5 share the complete unknown-time/type hypothesis roster and
unchanged prior. Neither exponent was fitted or selected after outcomes.

Every one of the 16 named final copied-stream endpoint-loss contrasts of a lower
power against one has its individual paired 95% interval inside plus/minus
0.02 nats. Four contrasts are stationary reference-family aliases, leaving 12
distinct cells. This supports bounded prediction similarity for these fixed
conditions, not simultaneous equivalence, all checkpoints or all evidence arms.
Every corresponding candidate-inclusion and set-size contrast is positive,
including its individual interval. Increased inclusion comes with larger sets;
it is not a demonstration of calibrated confidence or recovered provenance.

At 32 observations after a skill change, power one-half increases maker-state
loss by 0.18247 nats [0.13260, 0.23569], beyond the practical margin, while its
endpoint loss change is 0.01529 [0.01097, 0.01959]. Better set inclusion therefore
does not imply better proper scoring of the true maker. At 128 observations,
both lower powers improve stationary maker-state loss beyond 0.02 nats, whereas
one-half worsens purpose-change maker-state loss beyond that margin. The signs
and limits of both powers remain visible; no best exponent is selected.
