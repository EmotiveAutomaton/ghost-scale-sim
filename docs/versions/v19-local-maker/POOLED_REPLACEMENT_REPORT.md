# Pooled feedback replacement: independently verified

We tested whether discarding stale counts improves pooled tool adaptation. With 16 truthful forward-ordered inputs and original training, the mechanically wrong grouping gives the lowest changed-tool forecast loss among the six arms (1.26874 nats) but the highest original-tool loss (1.24071 nats). Correct grouping replacement gives a smaller changed-tool gain and original-tool penalty. Independent reconstruction, all 21,120 paired estimates and both complete replays verify this constructed-method reversal; prediction does not establish learned mechanics, historical process correspondence or human intent.

The complete paired comparison retains 640 endpoint queries, two original
training draws, original and composition-holdout training support, eight existing
development laws, both tool rules, both fixed feedback orders and truthful or
bit-flipped replies at budgets zero and sixteen. No model is newly fitted.
The three supplied group maps are complete input identity, correct removal of
the irrelevant undo-buffer coordinate for tool use, and an equally sized wrong
map that removes current-artifact identity instead.

Addition retains old empirical counts and adds each distinct complete input's
new report. Replacement discards old empirical counts only in observed groups;
both methods retain one unit prior per endpoint. Unobserved groups remain at
their pooled old counts. Conflicting distinct-input reports remain counts;
repeated identical-input reports deduplicate. This differs from the earlier
point-mass row replacement. It does not relearn the grouping or alter undo.

The truthful changed-tool means are smaller than the 0.02-nat practical margin.
Forward-order intervals remain below that magnitude; reverse-order intervals
straddle it. These conditional intervals do not support a general practically
large advantage or a universal negligible-effect claim. Original-tool forecasts
worsen in every listed truthful condition. Wrong feedback also appears better
than addition after the tool changes, while causing larger original-tool damage.
Discarding a badly outdated fit can improve a score even with misleading new
reports; score improvement alone does not validate the new mechanism.
