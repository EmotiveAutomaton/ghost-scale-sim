# Prior-mixture error and the value of cues - 25 September 2026

We tested whether a correctly understood cue can harm storage when the assumed source mixture is wrong. At two-thirds cue correctness, cue-guided storage performs worse than the same assumed mixture’s fixed storage choice in 671 of 5,880 mismatched problems. Independent rational reconstruction and both full replays verify this constructed-method reversal; it does not establish forecast accuracy or process correspondence.

Retained coverage is the probability mass of source records kept by a storage
mask. We froze policies under each assumed mixture before evaluating their
unchanged choices under every actual mixture. The cue channel was correctly
supplied throughout. All 28 finite mask libraries cross 15 assumed and 15
actual mixtures in quarter increments, under three cue channels. There are
18,900 distinct structural problems. These are not independent sampled worlds.

At two-thirds cue correctness, 5,880 problems have unequal assumed and actual
mixtures. Of these, 4,254 lose coverage relative to optimal cue use under the
actual mixture; 3,261 lose to the actual mixture's optimal fixed mask; and
671 lose even to the fixed mask chosen under their own assumed mixture.
The last comparison isolates harm from using the cue under the wrong prior,
beyond the error already present in the assumed-mixture fixed baseline.
All 420 matched-mixture problems per channel have zero regret.

With an uninformative cue, 2,870 mismatched problems lose to the actual-mixture
fixed optimum, while none loses to its own assumed-mixture fixed baseline.
With perfect cues, 1,400 problems still lose to informed actual-mixture choices:
zero assumed mixture weights make some labels nominally impossible. Those
labels keep the predeclared largest-mask fallback. Positive actual probability
on a nominally impossible label occurs in 3,696 perfect-cue problems. These
support and tie-rule effects remain part of the declared comparison.
