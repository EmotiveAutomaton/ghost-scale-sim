# Highest-probability retention: engineering disposition

We tested whether shared-law precision and highest-probability source retention could fit their complete V19 comparisons. Both implementations pass controls, but inclusive timing projects 4,094.10 and 6,866.40 CPU seconds against their separate 3,600-second caps. Neither scientific comparison was admitted. These are scoped engineering blockers, not scientific nulls or evidence that the week is exhausted.

This independently implemented alternative asks whether retaining sources with
the largest supplied probabilities preserves retrospective value better than
recency or temporal spacing at equal source count. Uniform, time-proportional
and reciprocal-time source weights are fixed. Equal-weight ties prefer recent
times. Seven rosters retain all, recent half/quarter, spaced half/quarter and
highest-weight half/quarter sources. Every omitted source keeps its original
probability in the uniform-maker-prior remainder. Count, mass and bytes remain
separate. Choices do not use endpoints, laws, likelihoods or measured errors.

The isolated admission suite passed 93 controls, including 37 new priority
controls and the retained weighted-source/runtime/portable checks. Independent
scalar Bayes materializes joint group/past-state tables, explicitly sums one-hot
loss and checks every fixture report/future coordinate. Fixed ranking, recent
ties, permutations, dropped weighted remainders, full/certain-copy/constant-law
identities and input/roster/source/forecast corruption are covered. The full
synthetic native fixture completes. No new scientific population was run.

Complete-support timing retains all three priors and seven masks, eight laws,
both evidence conditions, two paired draws, 28,672 posterior rows, 505,856 sources,
five copying probabilities, eight report endpoints and every future coordinate.
