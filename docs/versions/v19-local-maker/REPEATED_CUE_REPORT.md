# Repeated source cues - 25 September 2026

We tested whether a second source cue still helps when it can copy the first cue’s error. At two-thirds correctness, it improves storage in 174 of 420 problems when independent, 172 when copied half the time, and none when always copied. Mistakenly assuming independence harms storage in two and 27 problems at the latter two levels. Independent reconstruction and both full replays verify this constructed-method result; forecast accuracy and process correspondence remain unestablished.

The first cue follows a known symmetric three-label channel. Conditional on
the same true source, the second copies the first label with probability zero,
one-half or one; otherwise it draws independently. Both observed labels can
inform storage. The supplied source prior, mixture, channel and dependence are
evaluator knowledge. No trained reader or newly sampled observation is involved.

The 28 feasible storage libraries and 15 source mixtures define 420 distinct
decision problems per channel and dependence level. They are not independent
worlds. At two-thirds correctness, independent second cues improve 174 problems;
the largest added retained source probability is 0.041124. Half-copy cues improve
172, with maximum 0.020452. Always-copied cues add no value. Perfect and fully
uninformative channels add no value at any tested dependence level. The exact
gain is the break-even second-cue fee in hypothetical source-probability units.

Assuming independence is harmless under the independent law. Under half-copying
it does worse than one cue in two problems, with maximum regret 0.009284. Under
always-copying it does worse in 27, with maximum regret 0.033773. Regret here
means lost expected retained source probability against the dependence-informed
optimum. No universal numerical curve outside this finite population is claimed.
