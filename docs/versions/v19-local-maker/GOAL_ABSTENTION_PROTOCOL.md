# Goal-report abstention: prepared independent alternative

Can a frozen forecast reduce unsupported local-goal reports by declining uncertain
claims, and what useful coverage does it lose? This D-family diagnostic is prepared
but unimplemented. It uses the already accepted restricted and goal-product
forecasts, complete public operation witnesses and the same full paired roster.
It needs no favorable goal-decision result and no new fit or sample.

Fix costs before outcomes: an incorrect reported step costs one, a correct step
zero and abstaining costs 0.1, 0.25 or 0.5 per step. Compare always reporting all
coordinate modes, all-or-none reporting of the joint modal path and independent
step abstention. Each policy chooses its report using its own forecast expected
cost alone. At equal expected cost, abstain. Use smallest numeric goal for modal
ties. The all-or-none policy compares its forecast mean step error with abstention
cost; do not substitute joint-path probability for that different objective.

Keep logarithmic prediction loss fixed. Retain report coverage, correct and
incorrect reports per opportunity, conditional error at nonzero coverage, native
expected cost and zero-native-support whole reported tuples. Zero coverage has an
explicit undefined conditional error, not a zero. A partial tuple is compatible
when at least one complete native path extends every reported coordinate; evaluator
support only scores it and never enters the policy. Native posterior policies are
separate supplied-law rulers, not learned baselines.

Freeze all 704 packets, four arms and budgets, sixteen development lineages, two
draws and five seeds. Pair lineage resampling with seed 191012 and 10,000 draws;
keep training variation separate. Preserve exact choices, threshold equality,
probabilities, targets and raw costs. Require scalar exhaustive decisions,
all/none/partial-report fixtures, boundary ties, joint-versus-marginal objective
controls, source/reader checks, both complete replays and independent reconstruction.
The inclusive 1,800 CPU-second cap competes within D and the 80-hour exploration
allowance; the 16-hour reserve is protected. No user-value claim follows from
these stipulated costs. This is a constructed-method reporting tradeoff, not
historical correspondence or human-intent validation.
