# Cue acquisition cost - 25 September 2026

We tested when paying for a source cue ceases to improve storage. With a cue that is correct two-thirds of the time, acquisition has positive net value in 147 of 420 problems when free, 125 at a fee of 1/64, and 34 at 1/16; none benefits at 1/4. Fees are hypothetical source-probability utility. Independent rational reconstruction and both full replays verify this constructed-method result; forecast accuracy and process correspondence remain unestablished.

The fee is paid once before observing a cue. Choose either a free fixed mask or
a complete paid policy mapping the three possible labels to feasible masks.
Net utility means expected retained source probability minus that one fee.
It is distinct from CPU time, storage bytes and attained forecast accuracy.
The exact break-even price is optimal cue-informed coverage minus optimal
fixed-mask coverage; all paid/free ties are retained. The representative avoids
payment at equality, then chooses the greatest policy in lexicographic order.

The frozen population contains 28 libraries, 15 supplied source mixtures, three

174, 155 and zero; uninformative cues never improve net utility. Six perfect-cue
problems tie at a fee of 1/16 and four at 1/4. The 420 library/mixture combinations
per channel and fee are finite decision problems, not independent worlds.
