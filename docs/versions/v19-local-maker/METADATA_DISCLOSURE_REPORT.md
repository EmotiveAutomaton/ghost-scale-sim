# One-field disclosure: independently verified

We tested whether choosing which missing mechanic field to request improves endpoint prediction. Under supplied native laws, entropy-based selection reduces logarithmic forecast loss by 0.02823 nats on original tools and 0.04882 nats on changed tools versus equal random requests. Independent reconstruction and both complete replays verify this constructed-method advantage; reliable mechanical replies and law knowledge are supplied, so learned access and human intent remain unestablished.

Logarithmic forecast loss measures endpoint prediction error in nats; lower is
better. The table compares six request policies under the supplied native law,
weighted by native query probability within each of eight retained development
laws. Columns distinguish original and changed tools. Brackets are 95% paired
lineage intervals conditional on those laws and the complete query roster.

| Request policy | Original tool | Changed tool |
|---|---:|---:|
| No request | 0.14366 [0.13786, 0.14915] | 0.18483 [0.17892, 0.19108] |
| Always skill | 0.10474 [0.10096, 0.10840] | 0.14592 [0.14181, 0.15045] |
| Always belief | 0.05762 [0.05405, 0.06075] | 0.05762 [0.05405, 0.06075] |
| Visible-group entropy choice | 0.05295 [0.04920, 0.05625] | 0.05295 [0.04920, 0.05625] |
| Equal random request | 0.08118 [0.07753, 0.08451] | 0.10177 [0.09815, 0.10545] |
| Both fields, privileged ceiling | 0.00000 [0.00000, 0.00000] | 0.00000 [0.00000, 0.00000] |

Choosing a field improves loss over an equal random request by 0.02823 nats
[0.02753, 0.02887] for original tools and 0.04882 [0.04728, 0.05023] for changed
tools. Both conditional intervals exceed the declared 0.02-nat practical margin.
Compared with no request, the respective improvements are 0.09071 and 0.13188
nats. All requests reveal the actual binary field, not the endpoint. The full
conditional law used to choose a request is an explicit oracle privilege.

The selected one-field policy leaves multiple possible endpoints on 7.8851% of
the native population for either tool, versus 27.8448% and 33.7367% with no
request. Asking for both fields resolves endpoints exactly. Skill is requested
on 77.3337% of original-tool and 71.4419% of changed-tool native queries; many
of these choices are ties where either field is irrelevant. A high request
frequency therefore does not measure that field's information contribution.

Costs are stipulated nats per disclosed field, not measured human burdens. At
cost 0.02, the two-field ceiling has net loss 0.04000 and the selected one-field
policy 0.07295; paying for both is best among these fixed policies. At cost 0.1,
the one-field policy has net loss 0.15295. It loses to no request for original
tools (0.14366) but beats no request for changed tools (0.18483). The follow-on
allows declining a request when its expected benefit fails to exceed price.

Population changes matter. Under equal weights for the 640 retained queries,
the native-law entropy choice has loss 0.09958, slightly worse than always
asking for belief (0.09744), for either tool. Optimizing native conditional
entropy does not establish optimality under another population. Uniform legal
completion laws are separately retained: their selected policy has native loss
0.07571 and equal-query loss 0.09531. The full 2,304-entry regroup retains all
tools, populations, conditional-law assumptions, policies, costs and paired
contrasts. Infinite-loss mass is zero in all executed cells; the instrument
and fixtures preserve it explicitly when it occurs.

Every legal completion and reply remains enumerated, including groups and
replies with zero native mass. These use the declared uniform-legal fallback.
Equal random requests average the two proper scores; they do not score an
averaged forecast. No individual hidden field or realized endpoint enters the
selection decision. Reader exports contain 1,152 anonymous visible-query or
one-field reply packets. Conditional laws, targets, memberships, native weights
and choice maps remain in separate scientific/evaluator exports.

Independent reconstruction checks 221,184 native paths, 102,400 forecast vectors,
7,936 group laws and 1,152 score cells. Maximum discrepancy is 3.34e-16. Stable
scalar sums choose the other field in eight numerical entropy ties. Replacing
only those choices changes any audited score by at most 6.94e-18. This supports
score stability, not literal field-choice identity at floating-point ties; the
original source and choices remain unchanged. The separate tie sensitivity
record preserves every affected group and population score.

A second regroup starts from original score cells and reproduces all 2,304
means and paired intervals within 4.45e-16 using bootstrap multiplicities,
10,000 resamples and seed 190976. Repeated queries are not independent worlds.
No new fit, episode, training draw or protected confirmation lineage is used.
Parent native policy probabilities inherit their earlier bound validation;
this review reconstructs mechanics and mass sums rather than refitting them.

All 1,206 deterministic files match across original, adjacent and extracted-source
execution, each binding 536 source files and 21 inputs. The independent reviewer
binds 540 sources and 1,209 inputs. Original admission has nine isolated controls;
independent review has thirteen, including known informative/null laws and
deliberate forecast, choice, cost, law, reader and denominator corruption.
The previous 61-file regression's 247 Ghost and five Torch passes remain a
separate historical receipt. Optional-request admission adds seven controls;
its initial NumPy Boolean serialization failure was retained and corrected
before freezing, with no scientific outcome inspected or criterion changed.

**Warrant:** advantage for a supplied-law information-selection method in this
complete finite population, miniature — architecture untested. Exact verification
does not show a learned reader can infer the laws or that a human can reliably
report a belief or skill. Historical process correspondence and human intention
remain outside this result. This is exploratory, not untouched-lineage confirmation.

**Pursuit:** the optional-request comparison is implemented, tested and source
admitted. Independent prepared alternatives address lossless updating after
future-schedule grouping and noisy mechanical disclosure. Their protocols are
declarative until implemented and admitted. No tiny setting or allocation is added.
