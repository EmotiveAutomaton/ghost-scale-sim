# Routine and request revision: small pooled gain, conditional skill effect

Does adopting a changed request improve a fixed revision policy? The pooled success gain is 0.74 percentage points, below the declared 2-point practical margin; skilled presentation-purpose makers gain 2.52–2.80 points across the two routines. Native revision loses 4.31 points of net success versus keeping the artifact after stipulated costs. Independent reconstruction and both complete replays verify this constructed-method result. Explicit purpose adoption and privileged task optimization do not establish learned competence or human endorsement.

Success is the chance that the artifact satisfies the current request. Net success
subtracts the stipulated inspection cost of 0.01 and operation cost of 0.05 for
every non-inspect operation. Keeping has no cost. These are experimental choices,
not measured human preferences. Adoption explicitly replaces governing purpose;
it is never inferred from action. Local goals are selected before native actions.

For changed requests the pooled adoption gain is 0.738 points [0.659, 0.822].
Its full conditional interval lies below the frozen two-point margin. The native
revision gain before cost is also small, 0.769 points [0.676, 0.865], and its net
effect is a reversal of -4.312 points [-4.545, -4.074]. No practical pooled adoption
advantage is established. This does not make the skill-specific effect vanish.

For skilled makers originally pursuing presentation, adoption after the request
changes improves success by 2.519 points [2.245, 2.814] under the meaning routine
and 2.805 [2.514, 3.128] under the presentation routine. These conditional effects
clear the practical margin. This is a property of fixed native policies and their
tool access; it is not acquired expertise or a general skill-by-intent conclusion.
Function-purpose cells have essentially zero gain in this population. The model's
binary presentation symmetry remains a live explanation of that asymmetry.

The visible-task optimizer improves changed-request success by 51.129 points
with intact inspection and 26.129 with hidden presentation. It receives the maker's
skill and belief mechanics and an explicit task objective. The full-state oracle
adds exactly 25 success points (23.75 net points) when presentation is hidden,
and is identical when inspection is intact. The masked optimizer integrates
independent fair presentation bits for current and undo artifacts. That stipulated
prior and privileged mechanics prevent attributing these gains to an ordinary
anonymous reader, learned competence or human endorsement.
