# Randomized source-prior regret: prepared finite alternative

Does minimizing expected worst opportunity loss favor a different lottery from
maximizing minimum expected coverage? The deterministic decisions differ in seven
of 28 retained problems, with minimum-mass costs. Freeze the same finite library,
three prior vertices, individually feasible byte budgets and retained population.
No new histories, sampled seeds, training or protected lineages.

Enumerate exact rational basic lotteries with at most three masks. Minimize the
largest difference between each prior-informed unrestricted feasible optimum and
the lottery's expected retained mass. Keep all optimal basic solutions and the
lexicographically largest weight vector in ascending mask order. Retain each
prior regret, the minimum expected-mass cost relative to maximin randomization,
the worst realized regret and every positive-weight mask's byte charge. Expectation
over the lottery is not a pointwise promise and the prior cannot adapt to the draw.

Admission requires independent reduced-fixture full-constraint enumeration, a
known strict lottery advantage and a regret/coverage disagreement, identical-prior
and empty/full identities, all ties, permutations and corrupt inputs. Full-support
timing, original plus two full replays, independent rational reconstruction,
original-row regroup and documentary work must fit a separate 1,800 CPU-second
inclusive cap. Freeze architecture, capacity, evaluator teacher inputs and schedule
before outcomes. No reader inputs or fitting. Candidate-library method only;
forecast accuracy, learned provenance and historical correspondence are untested.

Prepared, unimplemented and unadmitted. Precision-choice stability is the independent
alternative. This protocol does not grant additional CPU or change the final time.
