# Exact forecast collisions: independent numerical review complete

We tested whether identical frozen local-goal forecasts conceal different native target distributions. At 2,048 labels, the predictive bank distinguishes all 704 public frames, while the frequency baseline merges them into 121–124 forecast groups. Remaining bank target variation is across laws, not across frames within a law. Independent reconstruction, all 4,464 paired estimates and both complete replays verify this constructed-method result; historical process correspondence and human intent remain unestablished.

The nine-number forecast contains three probabilities at each of three positions.
Exact binary64 equality defines each group; signed zeros compare equal and nearby
values never merge. All sixteen development laws, 704 frames, two training draws,
five paired fit seeds, four training budgets, four readouts and two forecasts are
retained. Native and equal-frame populations remain separate. No fit, new sample,
tiny setting or protected lineage was consumed.

At the largest label budget, the predictive bank, raw-history and frozen-latent
readouts each distinguish every frame in both forecast variants. Their within-law
target variation is zero to floating-point precision. Identical public frames
still occur under different laws with different native target distributions.
For the predictive bank, this across-law component is 0.00010576–0.00012838
in native-weighted squared probability units across the three positions. It
accounts for 0.93–1.64% of total squared error. Under equal-frame weighting,
the component is 0.00008108–0.00009751. These are descriptive sums over the three
goal coordinates, averaged across retained fits, with no declared practical bar.

The matched frequency baseline merges frames into 121–124 groups. Within-law
variation is 0.00037175–0.00116098 under native weighting and accounts for
4.58–13.73% of its total squared error. Nevertheless its total native squared error
is smaller than the predictive bank's at all three positions. Exact frame
separation therefore does not establish a more accurate forecast or useful
access to a hidden target. Group-average targets are evaluator-only references.
They were never supplied as reader inputs or used to replace predictions.

All 58 pre-outcome controls pass. Known answers include opposing laws and frames,
identical targets, singleton groups, one-bit distinctions, zero and unequal
