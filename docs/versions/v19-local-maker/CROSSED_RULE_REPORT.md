# Crossed supplied rules: identical coverage, different probability weights

Can a family containing each individual rule change still omit processes when the changes occur together? No process-coverage gain was found from adding the combined rule: a belief-state alias preserves all goal-operation possibilities. The combined rule still improves probability estimates, reducing joint process loss by 0.07178 nats with artifacts alone and 0.09211 with context; the full-witness gain is only 0.00224 nats. Independent reconstruction and both complete replays verify this constructed-method result. Supplied hypotheses do not establish learned cause invention or human intent.

Logarithmic loss measures the probability assigned to the complete three-step
goal-and-operation sequence, in nats; lower is better. Process coverage is the
true posterior mass with positive probability in the supplied candidate family.
Coverage of 100% does not identify the historical process or make probabilities
accurate. The table considers makers using both changed rules. Each row names the
available evidence. Positive loss differences favor the family with all four laws.
Intervals resample eight paired coefficient lineages 10,000 times, conditional on
these exact executors and native maker probabilities.

| Evidence | Single-change family minus complete family, nats | 95% conditional interval | Process coverage in both families |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Final artifact | 0.07178 | [0.06976, 0.07394] | 100% |
| Artifact and context | 0.09211 | [0.08847, 0.09578] | 100% |
| First operation witnessed | 0.04980 | [0.04822, 0.05156] | 100% |
| All operations witnessed | 0.00224 | [0.00203, 0.00244] | 100% |

The effect exceeds the frozen 0.02-nat practical margin with artifacts, context,
or sparse witnesses. With complete witnesses its entire conditional interval is
inside that margin. All four truth populations retain full process coverage and
zero abstention under the single-change and complete families. There is no missing
process effect for the proposed combined-rule comparison.

The pre-execution alias explains the support identity. For a skilled maker,
complementing its belief converts the inverted evidence repair into the original
repair while retaining the presentation-only tool. The joint goal-operation
possibilities therefore survive in the tool-only component of the smaller family.
The native goal-selection probabilities depend on belief, so their weights need
not agree. This is a finite constructed-method distinction between support and
probability, not evidence that an omitted law has been discovered by a learner.

The study enumerates 442,368 complete trajectories: four rules, eight lineages,
sixteen makers, four contexts and 216 three-decision paths per maker/context.
The independent checker reconstructs every transient artifact and undo buffer,
goal/action probability, public projection, joint posterior and all 172,032 scored
cases, reproducing 768 aggregate strata to maximum error 5.69e-14. Known-answer,
corruption, empty-support and belief-alias controls pass. Original and adjacent
and extracted-source replays match all 100 deterministic files; each timing
receipt is checked separately. No fitted model or protected lineage was used.

For narrower families, positive truth mass at predicted zero remains infinite
expected log loss. A finite contribution excludes that missing mass and must not
be read as a finite overall loss. The displayed comparison has no missing mass,
so its finite expected loss contrasts are valid.

Anonymous visible evidence is the sole reader export. Full laws, truth, raw paths,
joint probabilities and scores are separate scientific/evaluator material.
Validation cannot establish performance under an unmodelled execution rule,
learned hypothesis invention, another architecture or human behavior.

**Warrant:** exploratory constructed method; coverage ceiling and conditional
probability advantage, practically negligible full-witness gain; miniature —
architecture untested. **Pursuit:** no extra seeds or positive confirmation
promotion from a supplied-law advantage. Independent support/holdout adjudication
and the prepared routine/revision and transient-state alternatives remain open.
