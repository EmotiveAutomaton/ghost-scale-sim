# Fixed-bin resolution sensitivity: prepared independent alternative

Can the chosen probability-bin resolution conceal forecast errors even without
pooling task or artifact groups? This finite D-family method check is prepared,
unimplemented and unadmitted. It does not depend on a public-request result.

Reuse the accepted goal-decision forecasts and native references with all sixteen
development laws, two training draws, five seeds, four budgets, four readouts,
two forecasts, three positions, three goals and both native and equal-frame
populations. No request or endpoint subgroups, new fits or samples. Compare five,
ten and twenty equal-width bins with boundaries constructed as integer ratios,
including probability one in the last bin. These nested partitions are fixed
before outcomes. Do not select a bin count to favor any readout.

Preserve raw binary64 probabilities, bin memberships, joint mass, forecast sums,
native-correctness sums and empty conditional means. Compute absolute calibration
discrepancy at each resolution, its increments on each refinement, and the original
unbinned three-goal squared probability error as an unchanged score control. Finer
partitions cannot lower absolute discrepancy except for recorded rounding; a
counterexample beyond tolerance fails the instrument. Zero discrepancy in bins
does not establish pointwise calibration.

Freeze seed 191019 for 10,000 paired resamples of the sixteen laws, retaining both
draws and five seeds inside each. Report every fixed resolution and increment,
budget contrasts and normalized log-budget areas without a practical threshold
or architecture winner. Controls include equal and opposite within-bin errors,
native self, exact/adjacent nested boundaries, probability one, empty bins,
permutations, mass conservation, refinement monotonicity and deliberately faulty
bin assignments. Independently rebuild every array and parent identity; require
both complete replays and a separate direct-index regroup for numerical acceptance.

Admit only after executable controls and full-size synthetic timing fit an inclusive
1,800 CPU-second D card covering tests, failures, original, replays and review.
Retrospective updating remains the independent B alternative. No additional
family allowance or protected lineage is implied. Reader inputs remain separate
from scientific targets and diagnostics. The result concerns a constructed
calibration measure, not historical goal correspondence or human intent.
