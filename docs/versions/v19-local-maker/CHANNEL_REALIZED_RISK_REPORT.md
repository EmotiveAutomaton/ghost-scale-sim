# Realized-risk channel storage - 25 September 2026

We tested whether limiting every realized coverage loss removes a storage lottery’s expected regret advantage. At the optimal fixed-storage loss limit, 54 of 420 problems retain an advantage over unrestricted deterministic cue policies, while 93 become worse. Relaxing the limit halfway toward one restores all 174 improving problems. Independent rational reconstruction and both full replays verify this constructed-method tradeoff; forecast accuracy and process correspondence remain unestablished.

Expected regret is the difference in expected retained source probability from
the best library policy at the same cue reliability. Maximum realized coverage
loss is the greatest source-specific loss in any positive-probability policy draw
and label, relative to the best mask for that source. These quantities differ.
The reference remains unrestricted when risky policies are excluded from a
lottery. The interval of cue correctness is one-third through one.

The comparison preserves 28 byte-feasible mask libraries and 15 supplied source
mixtures. Three risk limits are applied before lottery optimization: the loss
of the best fixed mask, halfway from that loss to one, and one. The strict limit
retains a benefit in 54 problems, ties in 273 and becomes worse in 93 relative to
the unrestricted deterministic minimax-regret policy. At each of the two relaxed
limits, 174 improve and 246 tie; none becomes worse. Maximum improvement in
expected regret is 0.027697 at the strict limit and 0.061048 at either relaxed
limit. The 420 problems are finite library/mixture combinations, not 420
independent worlds; policy and seed repetitions do not enlarge lineage support.
