# Fixed-byte retention: independently verified

We tested whether equal memory budgets change which past sources can be retained. At the final 32-observation checkpoint, midpoint priority retains five fewer sources on average than recency under each shared budget, and predicts less accurately when copying has probability one half. Independent reconstruction, all 1,120 paired strata and both complete replays verify this supplied-law constructed-method result. Practical importance, learned provenance, historical process correspondence and human intent remain unestablished.

The two budgets are the lesser structural byte costs of the original recent
and midpoint half/quarter selections, computed before posterior weights and
outcomes. Those labels describe budget construction, not realized retention.
Recency visits decreasing times. Midpoint priority visits the original midpoint
selection in increasing time, then remaining times in decreasing order. Each
policy skips unaffordable sources and continues. Shared overhead, float masses,
integer source metadata, structural indices and one shared prior table are charged.
Each omitted source keeps its original probability in the uniform-maker-prior
remainder; the copied-endpoint histogram still contains every source.

At observation 32 of a 32-observation history, the half-derived budget averages
6,872 bytes: recency retains 14 sources and midpoint priority 9. The quarter-derived
budget averages 1,412 bytes: recency retains 7 sources and midpoint priority 2.
Midpoint priority leaves 60 bytes unused on average under both budgets. These
are averages over complete paired source rosters, two draws and eight equally
weighted laws; individual histories have their own structural budgets. The
mean forgotten probability is 0.682292 and 0.932292 under midpoint priority,
compared with 0.5 and 0.75 under recency. A ratio of mean counts is not substituted
for the mean of each history's retained fraction.

With copying probability one half, midpoint minus recency increases expected
squared prediction loss by 0.00000702728 to 0.00000829976 across the two evidence
conditions and both budgets at this checkpoint. This loss is report-probability-
weighted squared forecast distance, summed over eight endpoint outcomes and
averaged over future times and query contexts. Positive differences favor recency.
Across all fourteen evidence/structure settings at half copying, midpoint
priority helps in two settings under the half-derived budget and none under the
quarter-derived budget. At copying probabilities zero and one quarter, the
half-derived budget helps in three settings; at three quarters it helps in two.
The quarter-derived budget helps in none at any noncertain copying probability.
At certain copying all forecast differences are zero. All retained report
probability has supported forecasts. No practical margin or confirmation interval
