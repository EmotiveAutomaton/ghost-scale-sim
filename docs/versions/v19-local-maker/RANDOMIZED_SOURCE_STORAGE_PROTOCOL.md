# Randomized source storage: prepared finite alternative

Can randomization across individually feasible storage masks improve guaranteed
expected retained mass under uncertain source priors? Reuse the same verified
source rosters, structural byte costs and frozen finite candidate library. Every
realized mask must obey the original budget; an average-byte constraint would
be a different question. No new histories, fits, outcomes or protected lineages.

Freeze three prior vertices, both existing budgets and the deterministic maximin
baseline. A distribution over masks maximizes its minimum expected mass. Enumerate
basic feasible mixtures with at most three masks and matching active prior
constraints using exact fractions; retain all optimal basic solutions and choose
one by a fixed lexicographic rule. Verify nonnegative weights summing to one,
all three prior constraints and realized byte feasibility. Report expected mass
separately from the worst realized mask; randomization offers no pointwise gain
guarantee. No claim about a source choosing its prior after seeing the draw.

Controls: a known two-mask diversification gain, one-prior and identical-mask
identity, empty/full budgets, degenerate ties, prior/item permutations, independent
enumeration on reduced fixtures and rejection of average-only feasible masks.
Full-support timing and the original plus two complete replays, independent exact
verification and paired/equal-law regroup must fit a separate prospective inclusive
1,800 CPU-second cap. Freeze source, capacity, teacher labels and schedule before
execution. No sampling is required: all mixture expectations are exact.

Prepared, unimplemented and unadmitted. Teacher inputs are supplied evaluator
priors and byte costs; no reader inputs. Candidate-library decision result only,
not unrestricted robust storage, forecast accuracy, learned access, historical
process correspondence or human intent. Precision-choice stability remains the
