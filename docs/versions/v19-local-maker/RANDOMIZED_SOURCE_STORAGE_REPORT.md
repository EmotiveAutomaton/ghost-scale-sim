# Randomized source storage — 25 September 2026

We tested whether randomizing among byte-feasible storage choices improves protection against an uncertain source prior. Randomization increases minimum expected retained mass in 26 of 28 distinct problems, but every improving lottery has a worst realized mass below its deterministic baseline. Independent rational reconstruction, all 57,344 rows and both complete replays verify this constructed-method result. Forecast accuracy, learned provenance, historical process correspondence and human intent remain unestablished.

Minimum expected retained mass is the smallest expected fraction of source
probability kept across the three supplied priors: uniform, increasing with time,
and reciprocal time. The prior is fixed before the lottery is drawn. We optimize
over the convex hull of a frozen finite mask library; every mask with positive
weight must satisfy the byte budget separately. This does not solve an unrestricted
randomized knapsack or promise improvement after an adversary observes the draw.

Of 28 distinct roster/cost/budget problems, 26 strictly improve expected protection.
All 26 improving choices have a worse least favorable realized mask than the
deterministic maximum-minimum baseline. Two problems retain a deterministic
choice and tie. The enlargement of the feasible choice set guarantees no loss
in the optimized expected objective; the measured result is the extent and cost
of strict improvement. No forecast outcomes were evaluated.
