# Randomized channel regret: executable contract

Can a lottery over feasible contingent policies reduce worst expected regret
when cue reliability is unknown? Freeze the same 28 libraries, 15 correctly
supplied mixtures, interval [1/3, 1], structural byte budgets and population.

Enumerate every deterministic three-label policy and retain its rational score
line, endpoint regrets and deterministic minimax reference. Enumerate all
singleton lotteries and strictly interior two-policy weights equalizing endpoint
regrets. These contain an optimum of the finite two-endpoint problem: a minimum
of the maximum of two affine objectives on a simplex occurs at a vertex or on
an edge where the objectives meet. Retain all enumerated extreme candidates
and tied optima. They do not enumerate the continuum of all equivalent lotteries.
Canonicalize policy pairs in increasing lexicographic order; a singleton uses
the same policy twice with weight one. Choose the lexicographically largest
tuple (first policy, second policy, first rational weight) among tied optima.

Compare deterministic minimax, fixed storage and the two-thirds nominal policy.
Retain expected scores, regrets and byte costs at both endpoints and two-thirds
reliability; retain maximum realized bytes, coverage loss and policy regret
separately. Every realized policy and selected mask must fit its own budget.
No random draw is sampled; exact lottery expectations add no evidence or seeds.

Controls cover an exact strict lottery gain, no-benefit and point-interval nulls,
policy permutations, duplicates, dominated policies, individual byte feasibility,
all score-line intersections, and an independent lower convex-hull calculation.
Time the complete population and full synthetic libraries before admission.
The inclusive limit is 1,800 CPU seconds for implementation, checks, original,
two full replays, independent review, regroup and documentary write-through.

Independent acceptance must reconstruct ancestor rosters, costs and prior optima;
every policy, lottery candidate, tie, weight, expectation and realized maximum;
all 57,344 rows joined to 15 mixtures, paired and equal-law summaries; and all
source/input/environment/replay bindings. No training, new observations,
protected lineages, acquisition fee or new reader inputs. This is a finite
supplied-law method, not forecast accuracy or process correspondence.

Precision-choice stability and realized-risk-constrained channel regret remain
two prepared alternatives, independently of whether randomization helps.
