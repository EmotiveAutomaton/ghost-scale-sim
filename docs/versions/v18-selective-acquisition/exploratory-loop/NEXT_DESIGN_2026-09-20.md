# Retained next design: R3 calibration of the complete purchase process

Status: concrete reviewed next design, not implemented or admitted. This record
handles the post-P3 refill request without calling useful work exhausted or
extending the fixed science cutoff. P3 has completed and verified; a new purchase
calibration experiment needs its own executable instrument and frozen inputs.

R2 found that raw-surprise expansion bought more often when the supplied law was
correct than when the needed law was missing. Intrinsic predictive randomness,
initial uncertainty and the repeated opportunity to trigger are competing
explanations. Subtracting predictive entropy alone does not establish calibration.
The thirteenth sequential Terra research pass motivates assessing the probability
of ever purchasing across the entire stream, not only a single-time threshold.

Use the existing 32-unique-observation, three-order, four-cell distinct-law world.
Keep its equal-class and doubled-supplied-class prior conditions. Construct
128 separate calibration lineages per cell/order/prior stratum: 3,072 calibration
stream evaluations, with orders and priors paired within lineage. Calibration
truth is the supplied law. Freeze a threshold for each of two sensors to target
at most ten percent ever-purchase frequency in that finite calibration stratum.
Report the attained discrete rate and ties; this is empirical calibration, not
an optional-stopping theorem or a promised held-out rate.

The first sensor is R2's original four-observation mean surprise. The second is
four-observation mean surprise minus the predictive entropy of those observations,
computed before observing each outcome under the initial two-law mixture. Both
are past-only and use the same minimum eight-observation trigger time. Calibrate
the maximum eligible sensor value over the whole no-purchase stream; threshold
selection has no access to new-rule or evaluation cases. Entropy is an observable
model property, never a supplied true-law label. Both sensors may fail to improve
missing-law targeting at the matched calibration purchase rate.

Evaluate 20 new paired lineages in each of the existing four cells, four truth
conditions, three orders and two class priors: 1,920 evaluations. Preserve fixed,
selection, mixture, no-purchase and expanded-from-start references. Each sensor
drives the same paid-mixture expansion mechanism; retain the earlier raw thresholds
as contextual baselines. No reader receives the true condition. Deduplicate copied
roots and test copy invariance as a control, avoiding duplicate science arms.

Report held-out ever-purchase rates by actual truth, purchase times, sequential
and final logarithmic loss, native action match, all stipulated price sensitivities,
likelihood evaluations and actual CPU. Matched calibration purchase rate does not
match timing or refit work; charge both. All lineages and conditional strata remain
visible, including failures to target missing rules and outside-catalog truth.

Required controls before admission: frozen calibration/evaluation disjointness;
future-outcome noninterference; independent scalar sensor and whole-horizon maximum;
threshold/tie reconstruction; exact source-copy invariance; fixed no-information
likelihoods; behaviorally inert purchases with unchanged predictions and exactly
deducted recorded costs; independent mixture forecasts and native actions; raw
corruption rejection; all means and bounded extracted-source replay. No missing-law
advantage, nominal held-out purchase rate or utility ranking is a validity gate.
Calibration must have a separate bound artifact and verification before evaluation.

The existing eight R2 cold fixtures cost 3.15625 CPU seconds for full computation
and independent checks, or 0.39453125 seconds per case. Applying that reference to
the proposed 4,992 calibration/evaluation cases gives about 1,970 CPU seconds
(32.8 CPU minutes), before implementation, sensor-specific controls, aggregation,
replay and publication. R3 may have different cost; this is a planning estimate,
not a measured R3 runtime or a proof of minimum cost. No R3 pilot has been run.
At this post-P3 review fewer than twenty minutes remain to the fixed cutoff.
There is no validated basis for fitting this full design into that window, so it
is retained without admission. Narrowing solely to occupy the remaining clock
would not answer the declared calibration question. Work after the immutable
cutoff would require a separately authorized future allocation.

Other useful openings remain. P3 suggests a future-purpose coverage test for
partitions that are equivalent on the current five questions; that requires a
declared expanded purpose menu and independent controls, not relabeling current
equivalence as universal sufficiency. S2 needs persistent paid-channel error and
asymmetric-prior likelihoods; I1 needs joint-intervention training and equally
informed rivals; C1 needs matched-accuracy preparation and update accounting.
Selection interactions and recursive predictive-state training still require
new model artifacts. These are coverage limits, not an exhaustion finding.
