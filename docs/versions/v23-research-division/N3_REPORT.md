# N3: literal correction constraints leave missing support

Do literal correction constraints improve preference recovery? N3's primary comparison is unavailable in all 96 settings: hard constraints can empty the finite preference support. All 98,304 cases and complete replay verify; 62,209 half-space predictions are unavailable. No cases are deleted and the soft supplement is not substituted. This is a constructed-method limitation, not evidence that correction records contain no information.

The full first batch has sixteen whole worlds and 64 cases per setting. All six
retained rival fits, source archives, complete raw hashes, primary reconstruction
and distinct-process replay verify. The reporting repair represents unavailable
directions explicitly; it does not change the literal constraint or smooth its
predictions. The failed original and its reporting exception remain preserved.

At finite correction precision, edits can point against the true preference.
Intersecting all strict half-spaces can therefore eliminate the entire finite
catalog. Every tested setting has at least one unavailable primary world; the
family card is PRIMARY_UNAVAILABLE, with no nomination. Missing predictions
invalidate the complete declared comparison; they are not scored as zeros or
removed to form a favorable surviving-case estimate.

The proposed useful sample-efficiency ratio is not established for the literal
primary. Its failure under local fixes cannot be reduced to a finite average
loss comparison. Correction-plus-choice, coactive, exact and separately named
soft-constraint diagnostics are retained for exploration; none replaces the
declared primary or supplies a confirmed correction mechanism. The full
secondary interval calculation is replay-verified rather than independently
reimplemented in this review. No second burn-in is required after the terminal
unavailable card; its unused reservation is released.
