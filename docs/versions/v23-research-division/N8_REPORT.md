# N8: play signals and preference inference

Can public play signals prevent a reader from mistaking playful actions for preferences? In N8, the mode-aware reader improves preference log loss by 0.049–2.588 nats across all sixteen play settings; eight no-play controls remain at numerical zero. All 49,152 cases, both burn-ins and complete replay verify. This is a constructed-mechanism discovery, not confirmation or evidence about human emotions.

The nominated setting has play share 0.6, signal reliability 0.9, no threat
events and 32 observed records. Its pooled raw gain is 2.588047
nats, with descriptive 95% interval 2.328623–2.847471.
The calibrated selection uses a shrunk mean of 2.332766.
All sixteen nonstructural settings have descriptive lower bounds above the
0.02-nat margin. This supports the packet's finite prediction across this grid;
these adaptively reviewed intervals are not simultaneous confirmation tests.

The generator reverses the expressed binary preference during play and lets
threat end play. The reader uses observed mode signals and threat records while
integrating unknown reliability. The comparison ignores mode. The always-closed,
supplied-law and six fixed/fresh direct readers remain in the full record.
No actual aggression, emotion, acquired human intent or general superiority over
all direct readers is measured. The assumed mode law supplies the identifiability.

Both complete burn-ins cover 24 settings, sixteen law worlds and 64 cases per
setting/world. The first batch executes in four disjoint world partitions; the
second executes as one whole batch. Independent probability reconstruction,
canonical ledger round trips, proper scores, world means, raw hashes, frozen
fits and calibrated decision reconstruction pass. All scientific files have
fresh-process replay. No partial partition or empty/missing case entered the decision.
