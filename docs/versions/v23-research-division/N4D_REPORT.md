# N4d: what a maker sacrifices

Can observed sacrifices reveal how a maker orders incompatible goals? In N4d, two or six conflict probes improve true-mechanism log loss by 0.095–0.987 nats when stakes are visible and 0.050–0.928 nats when stakes are hidden. Zero probes preserve the exact prior, and hiding stakes sharply weakens recovery of the weighted rule. All 12,288 cases, 108 independently reconstructed intervals, six frozen rivals and full replay verify. This is a constructed-mechanism result, not evidence about human goal priority.

The zero-probe gain is numerical zero in all four mechanisms, as declared. Fixed
stakes favoring goal one alias the weighted and goal-one lexicographic rules;
varying stakes break that alias. Consequently hiding stakes reduces weighted-rule
gain from 0.403 to 0.050 nats with two probes and from 0.895 to 0.265 with six.
Stable goal-one and goal-two sacrifices remain identifiable without viewing stakes,
while the stipulated random rule separates more slowly from those fixed orders.

Controls verify the baseline and fixed-stakes aliases, the varying-stakes law
change, probability/log-space agreement, canonical ledger round trips,
evaluator-label exclusion and planted recovery above 0.9999 in every reliability
law. Independent extension review recomputed all 108 primary, secondary and
forecast intervals from 12,288 raw cases. Every source, fit, complete and replay
hash matches, including all six frozen rivals and their validation receipts.

These are finite binary conflicts with public stakes, two goals, paired random
menus and stipulated response noise. A finite menu need not realize both stake
orders; the exact reader knows the four candidate mechanisms and reliability
roster. Mechanism prevalence, richer goal trees, human interpretation, acquisition
and engine-module behavior remain untested. This miniature does not reopen N4's
resource-deferred main grid, is outside confirmation and does not admit goal-tree
functionality.
