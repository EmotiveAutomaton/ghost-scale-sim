# N14: pervasiveness, covariation and process

Declared before production under the October 17 packet. Constructed mechanisms,
miniature — architecture untested. This protocol tests identifiability under
stipulated response laws; it does not validate psychological diagnoses.

The curator's claim is: “The biggest thing to tell a branch that has been
autocompleted is the pervasiveness of similar-looking decisions. Other characters
being fearful imply that this character being fearful is about the author, not
about the character.” The packet's restatement additionally requires contextual
covariation, character-trait sensitivity and process traces. Both receive explicit
observation subsets; posterior ties remain ties.

Twelve settings cross genre convention (absent/strong), per-work attention
budget (8/24 decisions), and one/two/three works. Each work has four characters
with independently drawn persistent binary traits, three scenes and two threat
levels: 24 decisions. Characters are interleaved within scene/threat slots.
Thirty-two other-author binary genre responses have fear rates .20/.80.
No D30 marker delivery was found before declaration; these are declared defaults.

Five mechanisms: attended choices are fearful for fearful traits or high threat,
slow and revised, falling to the genre default once the attention budget expires;
scene habits are fast/unrevised, fearful in scene 0 and additionally scene 1 after
the first work; one-shot fear is fast/unrevised, fearful in scene 0 and scene 1
under high threat; deliberate global policy is fearful, slow and revised;
genre is fast/unrevised with the genre's modal action. These deliberately simple
laws preserve action aliasing of policy and genre in the strong-genre setting.

Joint responses encode action × latency × revision (eight possibilities).
Reliability mass goes on the stipulated response; remaining mass is uniform.
Sixteen law blocks balance reliability .60–.95; 64 cases per setting per block.
Mechanisms cycle across cases and blocks, retaining the slight 64/5 imbalance
and publishing confusion denominators. Adjacent mechanism cases share traits,
uniform draws and consensus draws. Cases are not independent resampling units.

Reader (a) sees only four character fear totals, pooled over scenes and works.
It integrates hidden character traits, genre and reliability using exact
Poisson-binomial count probabilities. Reader (b) sees indexed actions and other
authors' genre responses, integrating hidden traits. Reader (c) additionally
sees character traits, latency and revision. All know the budget/record design;
none sees the setting's genre label, mechanism label or actual reliability.
Independent probability products check log-space likelihood aggregation.

Primary: true-mechanism log-loss reduction of (c) over (a). Secondary: (b) over
(a), (c) over (b), and each reader's five-by-five confusion counts. Exact ties
receive fractional allocations; no mechanism is automatically labelled habitual.
This operationalizes pervasiveness as counts; it is not a claim that a human
must use this Bayesian classifier or the specified prior.

Six query decisions (one character, all scenes/threat levels in the next work)
are forecast from the complete observed history before query outcomes are drawn
into any reader input. The exact reader and six fixed/fresh direct rivals predict
the same eight-class joint responses. Rival training and validation namespaces
are disjoint from science. Query outcomes never enter the primary readout.

Controls: normalized laws; planted recovery under the strongest setting for
every reliability; action alias preserved and process information separates it;
probability/log-space agreement; canonical ledger round trip; evaluator and
future-label exclusion. Complete full-grid cost pilot/replay precedes six frozen
fits and one reserved 12,288-case batch. Whole-law-block bootstrap intervals,
retained raw records, complete fresh-process replay and numerical reconstruction
precede acceptance. Conditional result only; no confirmation nomination.
