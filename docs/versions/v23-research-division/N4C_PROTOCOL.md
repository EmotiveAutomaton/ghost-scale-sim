# N4c: distinguish a solution, a habit, an acquired end and habitual goal selection

Declared 2026-10-17 before N4c sampling. The owner's October 17 instruction
authorizes reasonable provisional choices while a further theory packet is in
preparation. This is an analyst operationalization, amendable prospectively;
completed N4b and the main N4 contrast retain their original meaning.

Source: Sounding Line's `THE_TRIPLE_INFERENCE.md`, the October 16 discussion of
G191/G192 and its afterword, plus section 4 of the October 16 revision-2 Ghost
addendum. The October 14 assertion that acquired ends never become terminal was
superseded. The present study does not test acquisition or establish human habits.
It asks whether explicit interventions identify four stipulated control rules.

The twelve settings cross four mechanisms with 0, 8 or 32 baseline observations.
Sixteen independent law blocks each contain 64 cases per setting. Reliability is
balanced from 0.60 to 0.95 across blocks. Baseline histories are exactly aliased
between mechanisms. Common random numbers pair their histories and probe orders.
These are constructed mechanisms, miniature — architecture untested.

Each response records both an observable goal-menu commitment and an action
(idle, original means, alternative means). A goal commitment is an observed
menu choice, not a report of a hidden mental state. Every case has six independent
reset probes, in randomized order: baseline, sham baseline, original need met
independently of action (A), candidate end satisfied (B), candidate end disturbed
with original means blocked (B), and original need active with original means
blocked. No probe trains or changes the simulated maker. All six responses are
forecast before any probe response is observed.

The goal-directed controller acts to reduce the original need. The acquired-end
controller acts to reduce the candidate-end deficit. The action habit chooses the
old goal and cached original action, idling if that action is unavailable. The
habitual goal selector always commits to the old goal but plans its means against
the original need. Planning chooses alternative means when the original is blocked.
An inactive target produces no goal commitment for the first two controllers.
The response law places reliability mass on the rule's response and spreads the
remaining mass uniformly over all six joint responses. This is declared response
noise, not a fitted loss floor; noisy responses may contradict the intended rule.

The exact reader integrates over all four rules and sixteen reliability values.
The primary descriptive readout is reduction in true-mechanism log loss from
baseline to all probes. A-only, B-only, baseline/sham-only, and action-only
(goal-menu commitment hidden) readouts identify which observations distinguish
which rules. No statistic selects a confirmation candidate. Six fixed/fresh
direct rivals predict the same held-out joint responses from public evidence.
They are forecasting comparisons, not mechanism-label classifiers.
