### D. One dial, as a diary would see it (G3)

**Whose idea.** The curator expects one dial: his Neuroticism is sensitivity to drops whatever the channel. The thread's rival: one sensitivity, plus a second input into anger, how readily a person contests others, because in the trigger table RAGE needs something to push on. Sounding Line measures it this week as a latent correlation across people between anger-type and fear-type reactivity, on an open diary set of 2,272 people (ES2) and on eight-day diaries (MI2). This family says what that estimate returns when the truth is known.

**World.** Each person has a usual level and, for each of two families of feelings, a reactivity. At each prompt a negative event happens at that person's own rate. A feeling is its usual level, plus reactivity times the event, plus a part shared by everything rated at that prompt, plus its own noise, rounded onto a short scale with a floor. Three items a family.

**Populations.**

- ONE: the two reactivities are the same dial (correlation of their logs 1.0).
- TWO: correlation 0.8, 0.5, 0.
- RIVAL: fear-type reactivity is the dial. Anger-type reactivity is the dial times a contest readiness that varies on its own, with a spread of 0.3 or 0.6 on the log scale against the dial's 0.5. By arithmetic those give correlations of 0.857 and 0.640 between the logs of the two reactivities, and 0.84 and 0.58 between the reactivities themselves, which is what the estimators target.

**Estimators.** (i) The cards' own: slopes on odd and on even days, the cross-half correlation divided by the root of the two odd-even correlations. (ii) The same from one set of prompts. (iii) A model with correlated random slopes, by any method the owner trusts.

**Cells.** People 300, 1,200, 2,272. Shape: 6 prompts a day for 10 or 14 days; one report a day for 8 or 16 days. Event rate 0.08, 0.2, 0.4. Floor: none, mild, heavy (two thirds of reports at the floor). Usual level tied to the dial: no, yes. Event rate tied to the dial: no, yes (V30's EXPOSED maker). Slopes on a yes-or-no event and on a graded one.

**Open.** For each estimator and cell: bias and spread; the share of worlds in which ONE is read as one dial and TWO at 0.8 as more than one under ES2's rule (lower end of the interval at 0.90 or above; upper end at 0.85 or below); where RIVAL lands; the smallest study that tells ONE from RIVAL.

**Fixed.** RIVAL's correlations follow from its spreads. Estimator (ii) is biased upward by the shared part by construction; its size is what is open.

**Pre-flight** (`pf_dial.py`, `pf_dial_daily.py`; 20 and 40 worlds a cell).

### S. Why a maker stays (G10)

**Whose idea.** The curator's seat for serotonin: "how much stress applies to your current solution set". The thread: it holds for the effect, and whether it works by lowering the weight of cost or by raising belief that waiting pays is open. Mice waited longer under stimulation at reward odds of 75% "[but not with 50 or 25% reward probabilities](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-04496-y)"; people on a reuptake blocker worked harder through "[a specific reduction of effort cost, and not by any change in the weight of monetary incentives](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.17282)". The handoff predicts the two accounts are "separable only when low odds are included".

**World.** A waiting task. With odds `p` a reward of 20 is coming; if it is, it arrives on each step with chance 0.15; the trial ends at 40 steps. Each step of waiting costs 1. As nothing arrives the maker's belief that it is coming falls. She stays or quits by a soft choice (temperature 0.5) on the value of staying, from an exact dynamic programme.

**Makers.** BASE, and four ways of staying longer, each sized to add 4.0 steps to the mean wait at odds 0.75 on trials where nothing comes:

- COST: the cost weighs 0.636 of its size.
- SHIFT: her prior that the reward is coming is higher by 0.650 in log-odds, at every odds. This is optimism.
- SHARPEN: her prior's log-odds are multiplied by 1.592. She is more sure either way. This is confidence.
- SLOW: she discounts the evidence of nothing having arrived yet (power 0.725).

**What the reader sees.** Each trial's wait and outcome. The reader is exact, and the rival's one parameter is refitted to the truth's behaviour in the conditions shown, so each rival is at its best.

**Pre-flight** (`s_stay.py`, exact).

| Added wait, in steps, at odds | 0.25 | 0.5 | 0.75 | 0.9 | when the cost doubles, at 0.75 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BASE's wait | 0.90 | 4.64 | 11.31 | 18.07 | |
| COST | 1.76 | 3.92 | 4.0 | 4.0 | 5.98 |
| SHIFT | 1.55 | 3.92 | 4.0 | 4.0 | 3.35 |
| SHARPEN | -0.47 | 0.0 | 4.0 | 8.0 | 3.35 |
| SLOW | 0.07 | 1.42 | 4.0 | 6.56 | 0.68 |

| Trials to 3 log-odds (truth first) | 0.75 only | with 0.9 | with 0.5 | with 0.25 | all four | 0.75 at cost 1 and 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| COST from SHIFT | 474 | 676 | 316 | 310 | 329 | 39 |
| SHIFT from COST | 559 | 799 | 373 | 416 | 416 | 65 |
| SHARPEN from COST | 559 | 57 | 18 | 12 | 12 | 65 |
| COST from SHARPEN | 474 | 70 | 6 | 5 | 6 | 39 |
| SLOW from COST | 1,917 | 138 | 44 | 30 | 28 | 18 |

**What the pre-flight already says (the analyst's reading).**

- In this world a lighter cost and a flat optimism are one maker to an onlooker. Both move the same threshold, both add the same wait at every odds above the floor, and lower odds help little: 310 to 416 trials against 474 to 559. What separates them is changing the cost: 39 to 65 trials.
- Confidence is told from either in 5 to 18 trials as soon as a second odds at or below even is shown, because its sign turns over there.
- The published pattern in mice, an effect at 0.75 and none at 0.5 or 0.25, is SHARPEN's row, and SLOW's less closely. It is not COST's row and not SHIFT's, both of which add nearly as much at 0.5 as at 0.75.
- So the handoff's prediction holds for belief as confidence and fails for belief as optimism. "Cost or belief" is two questions: which handle (the threshold or the evidence), and which form.

**Cells.** Odds sets as in the table. Cost at 1, and at 1 and 2. Reward at 20, and at 20 and 40. Temperature 0.25, 0.5, 1.0. Matched lift 2, 4, 8. Then sampled worlds of 25, 50, 100, 200 and 400 trials: the share settled at 3 log-odds and the share settled wrongly, since an expected count hides the spread.

**S.b, a maker who has to learn the odds.** Each maker starts a block not knowing `p` and learns it from outcomes. SHIFT becomes an optimistic start that experience wears down. Open: for how many trials into a new block each difference lasts.

**S.c, a second arrival law (texture).** Arrival uniform on 1 to 20, so the chance of arrival rises with waiting. The pre-flight's reward of 14 put BASE on a cliff (a wait of 0.73 at odds 0.75 and 17.16 at 0.9). Choose the reward so that BASE waits between a quarter and three quarters of the horizon at 0.75 before running.

**Fixed.** At one odds SHARPEN is a SHIFT. In a constant-chance world COST and SHIFT move one threshold.

**Route.** The theory thread, through the return: the two tables and the four readings above, marked as a miniature.

### M. Colored or white (the analyst's family; the handoff's S11 in a built world)

**Whose idea.** The curator: "Our temperature is colored, theirs is not", and "inappropriate emotions would be something that only humans display". Open between him and the thread: whether colored leftover catches makers and not pages. Sounding Line's EN1 looks for it between a sender's messages. Next week a model on the Spark reads the same pairs.

**World.** A maker writes messages at times from a bursty clock (gaps log-normal around 25 minutes), in threads that each add a shared topic effect, under a daily rhythm. Each message has a remainder: its departure from her habits after thread and addressee.

**Makers.**

- COLORED: a share `q` of the remainder is a state that drifts in clock time with a time constant `tau`.
- WHITE: every remainder is fresh noise.
- WHITE-IN-THREAD: fresh across threads, carried inside one thread. A model answering inside one conversation is this.
- PROMPTED: WHITE, plus a state someone else sets and holds for a stretch, then replaces. The curator: "humans doing things like reprompting can certainly create something that looks an awful lot like attention". Its steps do not fade with the clock.

**What the reader sees.** Times, thread identifiers, and remainders through a reader whose reading correlates `m` with the true remainder.

**Cells.** `q` 0.05, 0.1, 0.2. `tau` 20, 60, 240 minutes. `m` 0.3, 0.6, 1.0. Makers 50, 150, 600, with 100 or 400 messages each. Pairs: all; different threads only.

**Open.** Pairs needed to tell COLORED from WHITE. Whether the curve over gaps tells COLORED's fade from PROMPTED's steps, and whether gaps counted in messages do it better than gaps in minutes. How many messages by one maker are needed before colored can be said of her at all, which is the curator's question about makers and pages put as a number.

**Fixed.** After the rhythm is removed WHITE gives zero at every gap. WHITE-IN-THREAD gives zero across threads. So same-thread pairs can never be the evidence, and EN1 sets them aside for that reason.

**Pre-flight** (the EN1 block of `fixtures31.py`; 600 makers, 400 messages). With `q` 0.2 and `tau` 60, the correlation between consecutive remainders was 0.182 under 15 minutes, 0.119 to an hour, 0.041 to four hours and -0.008 beyond. A rhythm with no carry gave 0.114, 0.106, 0.101 and then zero until it was removed: flat and then gone, where carry fades.

**Route.** Sounding Line (`docs/exchange/`): pair counts for EN1's bar at the reader reliabilities it expects. The engine: messages per maker.

### W. Four settings (G9)

**Whose idea.** The curator's four seats, with the thread's amendments (the handoff's table). Provisional. The family asks the handoff's question: for each pair of settings, which observations tell them apart, and how many are needed.

**World.** A hidden context, one of four, that changes on a step with chance `v` (volatility). A cue each step that names the true context with chance `r` (reliability) and another otherwise. A drop on a need arrives with chance 0.10 and adds one unit of unresolved Stress `U`. A share `f` of drops can be acted on: a plan of four working steps removes one unit. `U` leaks 5% a step.

**The maker, one step.**

1. Reading. Her picture of the context is updated from the cue, knowing the world's `v` and `r`, with the held picture weighted `2(1 - a)` and the cue `2a` on the log scale. At `a` of 0.5 this is exact inference. Higher, the incoming outweighs the held.
2. Swap. She holds one schema. She keeps or swaps it by a soft choice over her picture, with a bonus of 1 for the one she holds and temperature `ts`.
3. Work. Her confidence in the whole chain is her picture's weight on the schema she holds; its running level (a fifth new, four fifths old) is `cbar`. She works this step with chance `sigmoid(d (cbar - 0.5) + 1)`, and the log of her response time has mean `-0.5 d (cbar - 0.5)` and spread 0.3. That is the level half of the seat. The handoff's seat has a second half, a burst when the chain's confidence rises. The grid adds it as W.c: on a step where confidence has just risen by `u`, the log response time falls by a further `d2 u`, with `d2` at 0 (the pre-flight's maker), 1 and 2. Open: whether a burst can be told from a level in a record, and what it takes.
4. Plan. With a plan in hand she drops it with chance `sigmoid(s U - 3)`.

BASE is `a` 0.5, `ts` 0.35, `d` 2.0, `s` 1.0.

**What the reader sees, four views.** V1: the schema she holds, step by step (through which family of actions she takes). V2: that and the cues. V3: everything she does (schema, work or idle, response time, plans kept and dropped) without the world. V4: everything, with cues and drops. V4 is exact. V1 to V3 need a filter over what is hidden and are marked approximate, with V30's check of an approximate reader against an exact one wherever both exist.

**Pairs and matching (2.10).** `a` against `ts`: matched on switches per 100 steps. `d` against `s`: matched on plans finished per 1,000 steps. The four mixed pairs: matched on the share of steps spent working in the right schema. Two sizes of move: the matched number shifted by 5% and by 15% of BASE's.

**Cells.** Nine worlds: `v` 0.02 or 0.10, `r` 0.6 or 0.9, `f` 0.2 or 0.8, and the centre (0.05, 0.75, 0.5). V4 on all nine. V1 to V3 on the centre and two corners (calm with clear cues; volatile with murky cues). Records to 5,000 steps.

**Pre-flight** (`w_four.py`: V4 only, moves not matched: `a` to 0.75, `ts` to 0.7, `d` to 1.0, `s` to 2.0; one world of 20,000 steps a cell).

- The pre-flight's maker has the level half of the confidence seat only (no burst).
- Every pair separated, in 2 to 219 steps.
- The slow pair was `ts` against `s`: 16 to 219 steps, carried in calm worlds by plans kept and dropped. It was slowest where few drops could be acted on (in the calm clear world, 86 and 219 steps at `f` of 0.2 against 32 and 72 at 0.8). A maker who throws schemas over and a maker whose Stress pushes her off her plan are told apart only where there are plans to drop.
- Pairs with `a` in them were carried by swaps read against the cues; `d` against `ts` or `s` by response time.
- **A warning the grid must heed.** Moving `a` to 0.75 raised switches from 2.7 to 19.8 per 100 steps in the calm clear world, while doubling `ts` moved them from 2.68 to 2.73. Unmatched moves compare nothing. Section 2.10 exists because of this.

**Fixed.** `d` alone enters response time and `s` alone enters plan decisions, so under V4 those separate by construction. `a` reaches timing only through confidence.

**Open.** Everything under V1 to V3. Whether `a` and `ts` can be told from the held schema alone in a calm world. How much of each pair's separation survives matching.

**W.b, the thread's amendment to norepinephrine.** "Jumbled at the top, tighter underneath": a maker whose walk inside a schema grows less random as `ts` rises, against one whose two temperatures are free. Open: the number of steps before the coupling shows in the spread of her actions inside a schema against her switch rate.

**Route.** The engine: which observation carries which setting, for its attention markers. The theory thread: the tells table's six rows.

### T. A strong worthless habit, or a value (G5)

**Whose idea.** The curator's ladder, Rounds 19 to 21. A habit has no payoff: "A habit is only tested by doing the opposite." A heuristic pays a little. A value feels good to apply for its own sake and she can choose not to. An identity is applied by default. One scale runs through them, what she would trade it for. A fluency has a readiness (which of its cues are present, and how much it has been used), a price and an effect.

**World.** Occasions. Each has a cue strength `k` between 0 and 1, a cost `c` of applying the fluency this time (zero on most occasions), and sometimes an offer `m` to do the opposite. Her attention is on the fluency with chance `q`; otherwise it runs on readiness alone.

**Makers,** one fluency each. Readiness is `sigmoid(b0 + 3k + 0.5 ln(1 + n))`, with `n` her uses so far.

| Kind | Readiness `b0` | Price | Effect when applied | With attention on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HABIT | 1.0 | 0 | none | skips at any cost; takes any offer |
| HEURISTIC | 0.0 | 1 | a small lift | applies while cost is under 1 |
| VALUE | 0.0 | 8 | a lift | applies while cost is under 8; refuses offers under 8 |
| IDENTITY | 2.0 | 64 | none when applied; a drop on a need when it is not | applies; refuses offers under 64 |

**What the reader sees.** Each occasion's `k`, `c` and `m` and what she did. In a second view, the effect as well.

**Cells.** Share of occasions with a cost: 0.05, 0.10, 0.25, with costs drawn from 1, 2, 4, 8, 16. Offers on 0, 0.02 or 0.10 of occasions. `q` 0.1, 0.5, 0.9. Two views. Records of 2,000 occasions.

**Open.**

1. Occasions needed to tell HABIT from VALUE, by the share that carry a cost.
2. Whether a reader recovers the two numbers apart: the error of the price and of `b0`, and the designs in which a ready cheap fluency cannot be told from an unready dear one.
3. What `q` does. With attention mostly off a VALUE runs like a HABIT, and the kinds differ only on occasions that make her attend.
4. How much seeing the effect shortens the record.

**Fixed.** With no cost and no offer nothing separates the kinds but use at weak cues. An offer to do the opposite settles a HABIT at once when her attention is on, by its definition.

**Route.** The engine's probe log (its Satiate, Switch, Remove and Price kinds): the mix of probes that settles a kind soonest.

### Q. Rank among neighbours (G8)

**Whose idea.** The handoff's theory page: "The two visible needs are read as rank among the people around a person. Knowledge and the body are read against the world." Comparison is how those needs are read.

**World.** 64 makers in groups of eight. Each has a standing that moves with her own events. Now and then the whole group moves together, and now and then she changes group.

**Two readings of one need.** RANK: the reading is her standing minus her group's mean. LEVEL: her standing against her own target. A trigger is a change in the reading, and a drop shows with a chance that rises with its size. MIXED: a weight `w` on RANK.

**Events that tell.** A neighbour's rise with her own standing unchanged (a drop under RANK, nothing under LEVEL). A gain to the whole group (nothing under RANK, a rise under LEVEL). A move to a stronger or weaker group. Her own events move both.

**What the reader sees, three views.** Her own events and shows only, which is what a page gives. Those with her neighbours' events. Those with group membership.

**Open.**

1. Shows needed to tell RANK from LEVEL in each view, and whether the first view can do it at all.
2. Under RANK and the first view, the share of her shows that have no event of her own behind them. V30's family L read such shows as leftover. A need read as rank makes them from other people's events, off the page. How often would a reader of leftover be fooled?
3. Recovery of `w`.

**Fixed.** By definition RANK ignores a move of the whole group and LEVEL ignores neighbours.

**Route.** Sounding Line: ES1's contrast e (envious answers upstaged with her own events held fixed) is the people-side of the first telling event. The engine: open question 2 bears on any reading of Stress as firings with no trigger on the page.

## 9. Limits

- Every world here is a miniature whose realism is untested. A count of trials or steps is a property of the miniature.
- A maker's behaviour is its definition. Where a result follows from the definition it is listed as fixed and must not be presented as a discovery.
- D's worlds make feelings from one linear rule with a floor. Real ratings have habits of use, missing prompts and events people choose to report.
- S says which accounts can be told apart by watching. It cannot say which one a brain uses.
- W's four settings are one way to write the curator's four seats. Another writing could move every number here.
