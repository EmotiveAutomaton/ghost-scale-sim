# N16 bounded-mixture pilot

Does a randomized information-seeking probe policy retain its advantage over a
fixed probe order when losses are bounded by a prospectively frozen probability
mixture? In four fresh balanced blocks, the 20%-randomized adaptive policy gains
0.066551 nats of true-mechanism log score over the fixed order after two added
probes. Every block is positive. This is a descriptive constructed-method pilot,
not powered confirmation, evidence about human questions, or engine admission.

## Result

The primary compares the correct-model adaptive policy with 20% random probe
selection against the frozen fixed order. Both readers are mixed with the same
fixed-order distribution at epsilon 0.1, prospectively selected on retained V23
data. One independent block contains all 48 settings, 16 laws and five cases,
including a complete five-mechanism N9 cycle for every setting and law.

| Fresh balanced block | Mean bounded log-score gain, nats |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0.063698 |
| 2 | 0.079807 |
| 3 | 0.062385 |
| 4 | 0.060316 |
| **All four blocks** | **0.066551** |

The four block means have a sample standard deviation of 0.008946 nats and
range from 0.060316 to 0.079807. The aggregate covers 15,360 cases. Four blocks
are too few for the separately planned powered decision, so no confidence
interval or confirmation verdict is assigned. The fresh mean is close to the
retained epsilon-0.1 planning mean of 0.064617 nats, but that descriptive
comparison is not a calibration guarantee.

## Disposition

**Accepted as a verified pilot measurement only.** The result supports retaining
the bounded randomized adaptive policy for a separately frozen powered design.
It does not turn the four blocks into confirmation, authorize the forecast 260
blocks, establish universal benefit under misspecification, or change any V23
finding. Full misspecification, tempering and selection traces remain in the
hash-bound raw packet for later design review.
