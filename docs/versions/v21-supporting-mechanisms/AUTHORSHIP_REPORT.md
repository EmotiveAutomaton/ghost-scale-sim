# V21 several authors versus one changing author

Can role records distinguish one stable maker, one changing maker and two makers behind the same artifact? Complete records identify every conflicting-aim history in this finite ruler, but aligned stable and role-conditioned single-maker policies remain indistinguishable. Equally informed direct lookup matches exactly. The single-person model excludes 29.93% of true histories. These are verified constructed-method results with supplied priors and exact source records, not learned authorship detection or confirmation.

The registered grid crosses two or three roles, aligned or conflicting aims, and no, partial or full role records. Eight independently drawn topology-prior worlds each provide 256 paired cases; the same topology and endpoint draws serve every condition. Each topology produces the same equiprobable binary endpoint. The last role determines that endpoint; earlier role outputs and adjacent-source equality may be observed. Partial records show the first one of two roles, or first two of three. The inference target includes the topology and all role aims. No true maker identifier or topology label appears in the reader evidence.

The population prior over stable single-maker, role-conditioned single-maker and two-maker types is supplied. Role-conditioned means that aims may differ across roles: an aligned episode does not itself show a changed aim. The equally informed direct table and role mixture receive exactly the same evidence. The single-person model restricts its catalog to the first two types. The oracle sees full role records, not the hidden topology.

Without role records, all three topologies have identical likelihood at either endpoint. With conflicting aims, the first role output separates stable from role-conditioned aims; a source-switch witness separates one maker from two. Three-role partial records already contain both witnesses. With aligned aims, source records can distinguish two makers, but even complete records leave stable and role-conditioned single-maker policies in the same exact class. More fitting cannot recover a distinction absent from these likelihoods.

Full records lower mean log loss by 0.98647 nats under conflicting aims (descriptive 95% paired-world interval 0.92989–1.02815), and by 0.53091 under aligned aims (0.45976–0.60043). The aligned residual is 0.45556 nats. The single-person reader assigns zero probability to the true history in 29.93% of cases in every cell, so its uncapped mean log loss is infinite. Source-switch evidence contradicts its catalog; its explicitly recorded endpoint-prior fallback does not turn that impossible conditional event into a valid posterior. The full-record oracle retains the aligned ambiguity too. All reader/condition scores, fallback fractions and paired contrasts remain in the linked records.

Limits: this is a purpose-built miniature — architecture untested. The exact catalog, topology priors and noiseless source links are privileges; noisy records and unknown priors are untested. There is no optimization, fitted-capacity or convergence comparison, law-marginal ceiling, execution shift or product-of-factors arm. Native-to-shift and duplicate-removal contrasts are therefore unavailable for every reader, not zero. Eight reused development worlds and twelve paired conditions are not fresh confirmation.
