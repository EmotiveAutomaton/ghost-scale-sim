# V21 copied evidence with uncertain source groups

Uncertain source grouping did not establish a reconstruction gain robust to source-label noise and copying pattern. With eight reports from four roots, random-copying loss changes range from a 0.00178-nat improvement to a 0.00036-nat deterioration, with every interval including zero. Stronger copy-removal gains occur when the supplied root count already determines the independent evidence. This is a verified constructed-method diagnostic, miniature — architecture untested; learned provenance and confirmation remain untested.

The hypothesis was that integrating uncertain copying assignments would retain useful information without requiring perfectly observed source labels. The finite original-law, native-execution grid uses eight reused development worlds and 64 current episodes per world. It crosses 1/2/4/8 reports with 1/2/4 roots where feasible, uniform random onto assignments or balanced assignments, and source-label erasure or uniform replacement at rates 0/.1/.3. All roots are used. Policies and interventions share current cases, root outcomes and graphs within each geometry. Different graph populations are paired within coefficient worlds. No case, report, repeated control or fitted seed is an independent world; there are no trainable fits.

Each independent root produces an 80%-reliable binary cue about route preference; every copy repeats that cue exactly. The four readers use the true roots once, integrate uncertain source assignments, multiply every report as independent, or project the current goal probability onto a feasible correlation envelope. The last is a conservative decision rule, not an inferred graph posterior or universal minimax rule. Generator law, root count, cue reliability and source-label error rates are supplied. The integration prior is uniform over onto assignments; balanced graphs deliberately challenge that prior.

Balanced graphs retain small mean log-loss gains; only their clean-label control and 10% replacement condition exclude zero descriptively. These unadjusted, correlated comparisons do not establish broad robustness. At 30% intervention the random-graph mean reverses under both erasure and replacement. The inferred reader sometimes beats the supplied-root reader on finite sampled means; this is sampling variation, not information beyond the oracle. Comparisons with the conservative envelope also retain uncertainty. [All world means, metrics and contrasts](../../../results/v21/provenance/PROVENANCE.json) preserve every reader and geometry.

The larger positive contrasts are duplicate controls. With eight copies from one supplied root, removing duplicates lowers loss by 0.01287–0.01566 nats. Here no assignment inference is needed. With two binary roots and every root represented, all-equal reports reveal two identical root cues and mixed reports reveal one of each; source labels add no further root-outcome distinction. With one report per root, naive and supplied-root likelihoods coincide. The independent reconstruction verifies all probabilities, clean-label and single-root identities, and the independent-root controls; 53,248 duplicate-control cases pass. Repeated degenerate rows are not new replications.

Full multiclass Brier is the sum of squared probability errors, separate from log loss. In the four-root 30%-noise cells, inferred-group Brier is worse than naive by 0.0000023–0.0001221, including the balanced conditions with better mean log loss; all corresponding intervals include zero.
