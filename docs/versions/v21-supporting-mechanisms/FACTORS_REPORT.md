# V21 knowledge, skill and permission

Can an outcome distinguish a maker’s goal from knowledge, skill and permission? Knowledge and permission are exactly aliased without a separating witness in this constructed world. Revealing skill improves future prediction more than revealing either of them, while the equally informed direct table exactly matches joint inference. This is a constructed-method result, not learned architecture superiority or confirmation.

The [frozen B06 admission](B06_FACTORS_ADMISSION.json) defines four fair binary factors: a goal, knowledge of an option, execution skill and permission. Intention is the conjunction of goal, knowledge and permission. Execution follows that intention with a world-specific probability of .55–.65 at low skill and .85–.95 at high skill; failure flips the binary outcome. These stipulated variables are not measurements of human traits or beliefs. Eight reused development coefficient worlds each contain all 16 factor states with 64 common execution draws per state. Cases, twins and reveals are paired within worlds; only worlds enter the 4,096-resample uncertainty calculation. There are no fitted seeds or confirmation worlds.

For each world we flip knowledge, skill or permission while keeping the goal and remaining factors fixed. This verifies that changed behavior need not reflect a changed goal. The 192 world/twin pairs are counterfactual controls, not 192 independent worlds. Reader row order is randomly permuted; actual factors, pairing and permutation remain evaluator-only. Exact witnesses reveal knowledge alone, skill alone, permission alone, or all three.

The table gives the joint reader’s results; the direct table has identical predictions. Rows vary the information supplied. Joint loss is uncapped natural-log loss for the complete four-factor state; Brier is the sum of squared probability errors over all 16 states. Goal accuracy is the modal binary goal decision. Future loss averages the true fresh-outcome probabilities under the same factor state, rather than adding sampled future labels. Lower losses are better.

An exact witness trivially identifies its own factor and removes incompatible states; the decrease in joint loss must not be called a learned inference gain. Skill reveals reduce future uncertainty more strongly than knowledge or permission here. Goal accuracy stays 56.01% in every information condition even though proper loss improves. When either knowledge or permission is absent, the endpoint contains no goal information; when both are present, imperfect execution still leaves uncertainty. Better factor reconstruction is not equivalent to a higher modal goal score.

Knowledge and permission enter the action rule symmetrically. For example, goal present/knowledge present/low skill/permission absent and goal present/knowledge absent/low skill/permission present have identical endpoint likelihoods for both possible outcomes. Repetition of that endpoint alone cannot distinguish them. A knowledge witness separates the pair. Independent enumeration finds 336 pairwise endpoint aliases across eight worlds; this includes other states with equal intention and skill. Reveals can separate specific aliases, but this does not establish that all remaining accounts become identifiable.
