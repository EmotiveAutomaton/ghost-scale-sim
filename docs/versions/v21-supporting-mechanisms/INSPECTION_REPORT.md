# V21 inspection records and selective absence

Does a missing inspection record show that no inspection occurred? Random absence does not; selective absence can instead favor inspection. At the half-missingness setting with 5% false records, modeling selective logging reduces log loss by 0.0873 nats, while the naive reader excludes 39.94% of true histories. An equally informed direct lookup matches exactly. These are verified constructed-method results with supplied observation rates, not learned detection or confirmation.

Eight original-law development worlds each contribute 256 shared cases. The 24 channel settings cross independent or inspection-dependent deletion, four missingness settings (0, .25, .5, .75), and three false-record rates (0, .05, .2). Current evidence is the artifact plus a ternary absent/negative/positive inspection record; there is no past history. The supplied law marginal is exact at the independent coefficient means. Inspection does not alter the endpoint in this world.

At nominal missingness .5, independent deletion removes half of both inspected and uninspected cases. Selective deletion removes 75% of inspected and 25% of uninspected cases. Thus an absent record leaves inspection probability at 50% under random deletion but raises it to 75% under selective deletion. These are exact channel identities, not fitted detection rates.

The naive negative reader excludes 28.17% of true histories under random deletion and 39.94% under selective deletion in this illustration; neither finite capped loss nor modal accuracy repairs its infinite proper loss.
