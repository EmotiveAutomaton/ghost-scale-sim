# V17 first progress: graphic procedure and memory baselines

Can a maker reuse learned procedures or concrete training episodes to solve new
combinations under an actual search budget?

The first implementation compares primitive search, the existing repeated-fragment
learner and episodic adaptation on a sixteen-cell graphic board. Training examples
and the construction goal are supplied to the maker. Familiar combinations join
trained pairs; new combinations mix individual cells; the changed-constraint
regime forbids re-touching an already placed cell while leaving the goal reachable.

**Constructed mechanism result: In the first graphic-world screen, episodic adaptation helped at the intermediate budget on familiar combinations and added cost without improving solve rate on new combinations.**

This is **miniature — architecture untested**. It concerns a constructed mechanism,
not human skill or artifact-only inference. Stitch, the hybrid, assembly and the
recipient/adaptive-reader families are not included in this first result.

## What ran

A discarded pilot used 16 histories across four constructor draws, producing
48 cases and 720 method evaluations. The prewritten budget-selection rule chose
8, 512 and 2,048 operations. The separate scored namespace then used 64 history
draws across 16 constructor clusters per regime: 192 cases and 1,728 evaluations.

Only 57 relevant private constructions are distinct in each regime: 171 across
the three regimes. Public cases number 192. Training-order aliases do not add
independent worlds. All constructor draws permute one motif structure, and the
three regimes share histories; none of these counts should be pooled as
independent architecture draws.

Each row below is one regime at the intermediate budget of 512 counted online
operations. Success is the fraction of retained tasks solved; costs are mean
declared operation/storage units. Cold cost includes acquisition, learning and
storage; repeat cost is one further deployment using the retained representation.

| Regime | Method | Solved | Cold cost | Repeat cost |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Familiar combinations | Primitive search | 60.94% | 539.31 | 507.31 |
| Familiar combinations | Repeated fragments | 73.44% | 510.23 | 425.23 |
| Familiar combinations | Episodic adaptation | 100% | 413.56 | 312.56 |
| New combinations | Primitive search | 100% | 408.75 | 376.75 |
| New combinations | Repeated fragments | 100% | 482.00 | 397.00 |
| New combinations | Episodic adaptation | 100% | 578.19 | 477.19 |
| Changed constraint | Primitive search | 100% | 409.81 | 377.81 |
| Changed constraint | Repeated fragments | 100% | 428.83 | 343.83 |
| Changed constraint | Episodic adaptation | 100% | 397.56 | 296.56 |

For familiar combinations, episodic adaptation minus primitive search is
+39.06 percentage points, with a descriptive constructor-bootstrap interval
[26.56, 51.56]. Repeated fragments minus primitive search is +12.50 points,
[-3.13, 28.13]; that interval remains unresolved. These are paired comparisons,
not comparisons between separate arm confidence intervals.

All three methods fail to solve at budget 8 and solve at budget 2,048. Those
finite observed ties do not prove equivalence. Storage is 0, 5 and 21 tokens for
primitive search, fragments and episodes respectively, under the same 32-token
cap. The second cost surface retains those actual unequal storage amounts.

## Checks and retained records

[Admission](../../../results/v17/setup/ADMISSION.json) passed 31 targeted tests:
28 V17 checks and three reused graphic-world tests. They cover typed expansion
and cost, missing/invalid probabilities, private-field rejection, withheld target
reachability, the actual interrupted/resumed command, a crash between chunk and
index creation, and refusal of corrupted chunks or changed locks.

and last case in each regime. The pilot received the same verification with its
separate counts. Bootstrap intervals were not independently reimplemented.

## What this changes next

Memory is a serious rival worth carrying into the parameterized-abstraction and
assembly comparisons. The present graph is too structurally narrow to establish
broad craft transfer, and the apparent search advantage has to survive
structural/order changes and total-cost accounting. The new-combination cost
reversal is as relevant to the next implementation as the familiar-combination
advantage.

The next work is the isolated Stitch setup and hybrid/assembly extension, plus
the independently executable recipient task on which B/C/D and the observer
bridge can build. The [implementation plan](IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md) records each
family's remaining work. These are pending implementations, not running jobs.
The campaign is open; its planning target remains approximately 17 September
late morning Pacific.
