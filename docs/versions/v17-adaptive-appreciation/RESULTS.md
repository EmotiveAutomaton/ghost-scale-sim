# V17: useful prediction, costly inference and reversals

Can a reader make better predictions by combining reusable procedures, concrete memories, a recipient model and selective computation?

**Closed 18 September 2026, with one retained apparatus failure.** All commissioned families have a final disposition; scientific verification, analysis and public products are filed. Closure does not mean every branch executed successfully.

Within these constructed miniatures, inferred recipient models improved predictions, a cheap empirical predictor beat a misspecified inverse model, and reliable feedback repaired stale-purpose revision. All three frozen fresh comparisons passed their practical bars. Benefits reversed under other conditions: misleading feedback harmed revision, and shared parameters harmed predictions for unfamiliar partners. One pooled-assembly branch ended in an apparatus failure.

These are **constructed mechanism results; miniature — architecture untested**. The verification receipts establish methods and provenance within their stated scopes. Neither kind of result establishes human appreciation, artistic quality, real-text intent extraction or a language model's behavior.

## Recipient and adaptive inference boundaries

In the assembly recipient task, inferred recipients also improve descriptive probability scores over retrieval: 0.3427 versus 0.4232 after recipient change, at repeated costs 8,938.06 versus 550.15. With a wrong editor model, the corresponding scores are 0.3531 versus 0.5320. Actual legal assembly states and edits generate these outcomes; the supplied-recipient condition remains an assisted ceiling.

Adaptive strategies do not dominate. After twelve observations with a misleading old cue, fixed inverse inference scores 0.1660, the empirical reader 0.3096, confidence-only refinement 1.0271, and the learned controller 0.2480. Under noisy evidence, confidence refinement instead scores 0.0232 versus fixed inverse's 0.1705. Confidence can therefore be useful within one noise construction and badly misleading after a cue reversal.

The controller's cheap repeated predictions also carry substantial development cost. In the fresh accidentally-correct comparison, it costs about 7.56 million cold units and 190.25 repeated units, compared with the empirical reader's 282.67 and 246.67. Its Brier score is 0.2131 versus the empirical reader's 0.0897, even though both make nearly every maximum-probability choice correctly. Equal choice accuracy is not equal calibration or equal forecast quality. Controller development is frozen and charged; it is not free inference.

## Revision, observation and partial sharing

Reliable feedback helps when remembered purpose is stale, and it harms when the feedback is misleading. In the latter robustness condition, feedback succeeds on 16.62% versus equal-cost internal reconsideration's 58.38%. Under stable purpose both achieve 58.40%; that observed equality is descriptive, not a formal equivalence test. The goal-informed monitor remains a strong rival, and stopping without reconsideration fails many available improvements.

More evidence has different value across families. The construction artifact collection changes no forecast benefit here while increasing repeated inference cost from 4,249.38 to 8,445.81. Process evidence then helps. Revision's finite policy family is resolved by the earlier-object collection, but this does not recover every historical stochastic action. These readers compare against cheaper surface and episodic continuations; their advantages do not establish that a latent representation is necessary against every equally informed bounded direct implementation.

The commissioned partial-sharing extension separates partner prediction from the performer's own execution. With the modeled expert partner, shared, separate and partial models respectively score 0.1122, 0.1518 and 0.1198 on partner prediction. With an unfamiliar partner, shared parameters reverse badly: 1.1698 versus separate 0.2280 and partial 0.2582. Actual personal-goal success in the unfamiliar condition is 87.17%, 88.02% and 87.76%, respectively. Prediction and execution are separate outcomes; partial sharing mitigates interference without beating separation in this condition. Partial inference also costs more.
