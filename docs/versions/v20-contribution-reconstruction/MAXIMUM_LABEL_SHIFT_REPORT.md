# V20 maximum-label shifts and first shifted-context candidate

We tested whether the largest label budget lets structured reading outperform matched direct readers under changed rules. One of twenty comparisons passes the discovery criterion: context plus four past episodes under the changed execution rule. The other nineteen fail. All 9,175,040 forecasts verify, and the complete candidate replays exactly. This is a constructed-method discovery result; confirmation remains untouched.

Every new condition uses 131,072 training labels. Context spans zero, one, four, sixteen and sixty-four past episodes under both shifts; the other conditions cover artifact, sparse and complete records with no history, and artifact/sparse records with one past episode. There are now 362 verified packets. Among 92 reviewed first-fit shifts, one context condition is a discovery candidate and 91 fail. The earlier native primary failures remain unchanged. The candidate is a selected shifted condition, not evidence of a general advantage or a replacement native primary.

Training examples are native and byte-identical to each verified native counterpart. Current and retained episodes both use the shifted rule. The execution change reverses revision repair under both proposal routes; it is not a tool-specific skill manipulation. Every reader receives exact legal support from the shifted supplied law. This tests transfer under supplied constraints, not discovery of an unknown law. Complete observed records still withhold adopted aims, and inspection remains latent in these learned cards.

The uncertainty unit is a whole coefficient world: 32 worlds, one fit per new condition. Native and shifted evaluation samples differ; rows are not paired or independent uncertainty units. Repeated fits must be averaged within worlds. Intervals use 4,096 fixed-seed whole-world resamples and remain descriptive, conditional on the finite generator and shared training worlds.

Capped joint log loss is minus the natural logarithm of probability assigned to the true seven-part answer, with a probability floor of one trillionth; lower is better. The frozen advantage criterion requires a mean improvement of at least 0.02 natural-log units against both direct rivals and the legal template, a positive lower descriptive world interval, and no increase in wrong attribution at 90% confidence. This capped score is distinct from proper squared probability loss, and exact-zero truth mass is reported separately.
