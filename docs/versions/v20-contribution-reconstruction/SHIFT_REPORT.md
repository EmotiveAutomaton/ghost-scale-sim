# V20 execution and presentation shift review

We tested whether structured reading transfers across changed execution and presentation rules. All 41 comparisons fail the declared advantage criterion. The presentation change worsens structured context reading in all six matched conditions. All 18,808,832 new forecasts verify. These are constructed-method results; confirmation remains untouched.

The 41 conditions include twelve context comparisons and 29 other evidence-tier diagnostics, with zero, four or 64 retained episodes and 8,192 or 32,768 training labels. The batch ends before the final seven matched shift conditions complete; their outcomes remain pending. There are 273 verified packets overall. Earlier native repeated-fit results remain unchanged: thirty failed context conditions through 32,768 labels and 57 failed evidence-tier diagnostics. Counts overlap conditions across fits; packets are not independent hypotheses.

Training uses the original law. The execution challenge reverses dependency repair for both proposal routes; it is a global execution-law change, not a tool-specific skill effect. The presentation challenge reverses the presentation result when revision occurs. Both current and retained episodes use the shifted law. All learned readers receive common legal support computed from that shifted law. This is transfer with supplied constraints, not successful discovery of an unknown law.

Each comparison pairs readers inside the same 32 discovery worlds. Native-versus-shift contrasts pair those whole worlds and verify byte-identical training examples. Their evaluation samples differ, so rows are not paired. There is one fit in each new shift condition; the two-fit native analyses are separate, with repeated fits nested within worlds. Descriptive 95% intervals use a fixed 4,096-resample world bootstrap conditional on shared training worlds.

The declared advantage criterion requires at least 0.02 natural-log units improvement against direct frequencies, the joint neural reader and legal template, a positive lower descriptive world interval, and no increase in wrong attribution at confidence of at least 90%. Other evidence tiers cannot replace the context comparison. Capped joint log loss is minus the natural logarithm of the true seven-part answer probability, floored at one trillionth; lower is better. It is not an uncapped proper score.
