# V20 repeated-fit context shifts

We tested whether repeating training preserves a structured-reader advantage after rule changes with four past episodes and 32,768 labels. Both paired-fit context comparisons fail. All 917,504 new forecasts verify, with no exact-zero truth probabilities. These are constructed-method results; confirmation remains untouched.

The two new packets are the second registered fits for execution and presentation changes at four retained episodes and 32,768 training labels. Their first fits were already independently verified. There are now 384 verified packets. These are context-tier discovery comparisons. The earlier candidate uses 131,072 labels and remains a separate configuration; this lower-budget failure neither confirms nor overturns it. The campaign remains open.

Each fit is averaged inside its coefficient world before comparing readers across 32 whole worlds, using 4,096 fixed-seed descriptive bootstrap resamples. Neither two fits nor 131,072 evaluation rows per reader create additional independent worlds. Results remain conditional on the shared training-world allocation. The source, model settings and criteria are unchanged.

Capped joint log loss is minus the natural logarithm of probability assigned to the true seven-part answer, floored at one trillionth; lower is better. The discovery rule requires improvement of at least 0.02 natural-log units against both direct rivals and the legal template, a positive lower descriptive world interval, and no increase in incorrect attribution at 90% confidence. The floor makes this a capped score; squared probability loss remains proper.
