# V20 longer-history context shifts

We tested whether longer histories let structured context reading retain an advantage under changed execution or presentation rules. All five new comparisons fail the declared advantage criterion. All 2,293,760 new forecasts verify. These are constructed-method results; confirmation remains untouched.

The new comparisons cover both shifts with 16 retained episodes and 32,768 training labels, both shifts with 256 episodes and 8,192 labels, and the execution-law change with 256 episodes and 32,768 labels. The matched presentation condition at that final budget is already admitted and remains pending. There are now 323 verified packets. All 53 reviewed first-fit shifted conditions fail, including 17 context conditions. This extends the tested failure region; it does not establish universal failure of structured methods.

Training data are native and byte-identical to each matched native first-fit counterpart. Both current and retained episodes use the shifted law. The execution challenge reverses dependency repair under both proposal routes; the presentation challenge reverses the presentation result when revision occurs. Readers receive common exact legal support from the shifted law. This is transfer under supplied constraints, not recovery of an unknown law.

Capped joint log loss is minus the natural logarithm of the probability of the true seven-part answer, with probability floored at one trillionth; lower is better. A declared advantage requires at least 0.02 natural-log units improvement over direct frequencies, the joint neural reader and the legal template, a positive lower world interval, and no increase in wrong attribution at confidence of at least 90%. Exact-zero truth mass is checked separately from this capped score. Squared probability loss remains a proper score.

Readers and native-versus-shift effects are paired inside each of 32 whole coefficient worlds. One fit is present in each new condition; repeated fits are nested inside worlds, not independent observations. Native and shifted evaluation samples differ, so rows are not paired. Descriptive 95% intervals use 4,096 fixed-seed world-bootstrap resamples, conditional on shared training worlds.

The presentation change worsens structured reading in both new matched conditions. The execution-law change lowers its loss in all three matched conditions, but each still loses to all three required rivals. Lower loss relative to an already poor native condition does not establish a reader advantage.

At 256 episodes and 8,192 training labels, wrong attributions made with at least 90% confidence occur in 41.01% of cases under the execution change and 56.55% under the presentation change. At 32,768 labels under the execution change, that fraction is 10.59%. These are descriptive world-averaged rates, not independent-row uncertainty estimates. No exact-zero probabilities are needed for these failures.
