# Ghost Scale Simulation — V4 Specification

**Author of spec:** Abraham Haskins, PhD
**Target:** an autonomous coding agent, extending the existing `ghost-scale-sim` repository
**Depends on:** V1–V3 specs, `RESULTS.md`, `RESULTS_V2.md`, `RESULTS_V3.md`, `DECISIONS_V2.md`,
`DECISIONS_V3.md`, and the existing codebase.

V4 is not a repair. V1–V3 fixed instruments. V4 changes what the model claims the operative
variable is, on theoretical grounds, and then tests whether the existing results survive the
change. **If they do not survive, that is the finding and it gets reported.**

---

## 0. The reframe

### 0.1 What V1–V3 implemented

Synthetic content was modeled as **goal-empty**. `noise_free_synth` is structured, non-uniform,
and *goal-independent*: mutual information between features and goal goes to zero. The
Ghost Scale tiers mixed a goal signature against that goal-independent distribution:

```
A[0][:, tier, goal, DEEP] = alpha[tier] · sig[goal] + (1 - alpha[tier]) · noise_free_synth
```

The crash therefore arises from **unidentifiability**: no goal explains the observations better
than any other, expected information gain goes to zero, the observer disengages.

### 0.2 Why that is the wrong variable

Generative models are not goal-empty. They are optimizing something, and their outputs carry
real structure produced by a real policy. What they lack is not a goal but a **human-shaped**
one. The observer's inference machinery works by assuming the creator is a fellow agent with a
body plan, sensory apparatus, and action space resembling its own; that shared structure is what
collapses an otherwise ill-posed inverse problem into a tractable one.

So the operative variable is not *how much goal signal is present*. It is **whether the
generating policy lies inside the observer's hypothesis space.**

Two arguments force this, and both come from the framework rather than from convenience:

**The nesting argument.** A genuinely low-intent human artifact is nearly unobtainable. A casual
mark is produced by a policy shaped by decades of prior decisions; the artifact inherits that
structure whether or not the moment of production was deliberate. Human α has a floor well
above zero.

**The epistemic-foraging argument.** Under the FEP, exploration is a policy — it is the epistemic
value term of expected free energy. An observer modeling a creator as a fellow FEP agent
therefore always has *"they were reducing uncertainty"* available as a valid latent goal. This is
not a failure to converge. It is convergence on a real goal, and it covers an enormous fraction
of low-stakes human production.

Together these mean **there is no human condition with no recoverable goal.** The proposed
"low-intent human artifact" cell does not exist, and the human/synthetic confound cannot be
broken by adding it. It has to be broken by changing the variable.

### 0.3 What V4 implements instead

Synthetic content becomes **goal-foreign**: generated by an actual policy over an actual goal,
where that goal and its feature signature lie partly or wholly outside the observer's likelihood
family. A continuous **overlap parameter ω ∈ [0, 1]** governs how much of the creator's
generative structure the observer's hypothesis space can express.

- **ω = 1** — creator and observer share a likelihood family. Human.
- **ω = 0** — the creator's structure is inexpressible in the observer's family. Fully foreign.
- **0 < ω < 1** — partial overlap. This is where curation, hybrid production, and any
  partially-shared architecture live.

The crash now arises from **misspecification** rather than unidentifiability: the observer
explains out-of-family structure using in-family hypotheses, which is a mechanism that predicts
*confident wrong answers* directly rather than requiring a separate story about why a flat
likelihood produces confabulation.

**This subsumes three previously separate proposals.** The morphological-distance condition, the
curation condition, and the confound are all sweeps of ω. There is no separate animal experiment
and none should be built; the empirical version is unfalsifiable by the author's own assessment
and the simulation version is this parameter.

### 0.4 What must be preserved

**V1–V3 results are not superseded until V4 shows they fail.** The generative-crash, trust-exploit,
expertise, and labeling-coverage results were produced under the unidentifiability model. V4 must
demonstrate at its boundary conditions that it reproduces them, or explain why not. See §3.

## 1. Model changes

### C1 — Hypothesis-space overlap replaces intent dilution

The observer's likelihood family is defined by its goal signatures `sig_i[g]`, `g ∈ 0..G-1`, over
`F` features. Define the observer's **expressible set** as the convex hull of those signatures.

A creator has its own goal set and its own signatures. Construct a foreign signature family
`sig_foreign[k]` over the same `F` features but with support and structure the observer's family
cannot reach:

```
sig_foreign[k] = normalize( (1 - ω) · foreign_basis[k] + ω · sig_true[k] )
```

where `foreign_basis[k]` is drawn once, deterministically, from a low-concentration Dirichlet
**restricted to a feature partition disjoint from the dominant support of `sig_true`**, so that
the foreign family is structured, goal-dependent, and genuinely out-of-family rather than merely
different.

**Three properties the construction must satisfy, and each gets an assertion:**

1. `MI(features; foreign_goal)` under the *foreign* family is **high**. Foreign content is
   goal-directed. This is the entire point and it is the opposite of `noise_free_synth`.
2. `MI(features; observer_goal)` under the *observer's* family, evaluated on foreign content, is
   **low**. The observer cannot read it.
3. Feature entropy of foreign content is **low**. It is structured, not noise. Same requirement
   V1 §9 N6 imposed, carried forward.

Property 1 is new and it is what distinguishes V4 from V3. Assert it explicitly; a foreign family
that is not itself goal-informative has reverted to the old model.

`alpha` per tier is **retained** as the declared-tier mapping (see C4) but is no longer identical
to ω. The α ↔ opacity correspondence stops being an assumption baked into the likelihood and
becomes a hypothesis with an error term.

### C2 — The epistemic-foraging goal

Add a `G+1`'th goal to the **observer's** hypothesis space: `EXPLORE`.

**Its signature must be pre-registered before any run, and the construction is not free:**

```
sig_EXPLORE = normalize( mean_g( sig_i[g] ) )
```

The uniform mixture over the observer's own goal signatures. **Human-shaped but goal-agnostic.**
This is the principled construction, not a convenience: under the FEP, when epistemic value
dominates pragmatic value, the policy distribution flattens *within the agent's policy space*,
not globally. An exploring human still moves like a human.

Note what this is not. It is **not** uniform over features. A uniform-over-features EXPLORE would
absorb anything, including noise, and would trivially destroy every result. Write the
construction to `results/v4_explore_prereg.json` before the sweep and assert at runtime that the
constructed signature matches.

**Why this is the decisive experiment.** The V1–V3 crash could be an artifact of an artificially
small hypothesis space: the observer had four specific goals and no fallback, so of course it
failed to explain foreign content. EXPLORE is the most generous fallback the theory permits. If
the crash survives it, the result is far stronger than anything so far. If EXPLORE absorbs foreign
content, the crash was an artifact of hypothesis-space poverty and **the framework has a serious
problem that must be reported as such.**

### C3 — κ as inferred honesty, with source reputation

κ stops being a dial and becomes a posterior.

Add a hidden state factor **per named source**: `source_honesty ∈ {honest, dishonest}`. The
observer maintains a belief over each source's honesty, carried across encounters, and updates it
from the mismatch between the declared tier and provenance inferred from content. Content-based
provenance inference already exists (`A[0]` differs across provenance), so the machinery for
detecting a mismatch is present.

```
kappa_source = P(honest | encounter history with that source)
```

**What this unlocks that V1–V3 could not touch: Appendix B.** The preprint's Zahavian signaling
section is entirely about asymmetric reputational cost and the 95% tier as a strict commitment
device. **The simulation has never had a reputation mechanism, so the game-theoretic core of the
framework has never been tested.** With honesty inferred over repeated encounters, it becomes
testable: a source can build trust and defect, defection can be detected at some latency, and the
commitment device can be shown to work or not.

Retain fixed-κ as a configuration option so V1–V3 experiments remain runnable unchanged.

### C4 — Declared tier and actual overlap decouple

Currently the Ghost Scale tier *is* the intent density, so misapplication of the scale is
literally unrepresentable. In deployment, misapplication is guaranteed — honestly, constantly,
at scale.

Separate them:

- `declared_tier ∈ {CREATOR, POLISHED, CURATOR, GHOST}` — what the artifact claims.
- `omega_true ∈ [0, 1]` — what the artifact actually is.
- `calibration_error = |alpha[declared_tier] - omega_true|`

The α ↔ opacity correspondence becomes the claim that calibration error is small, which is now a
measurable quantity rather than a definition. **A scale that only works when applied perfectly is
a wish, not a proposal.** This change is what lets the model say anything about deployment.

### C5 — Curation as relocated structure

Partition the feature space into an **execution** subspace and a **selection** subspace.

- A crafted human artifact carries goal structure in execution features.
- A curated artifact carries little goal structure in execution features and substantial goal
  structure in selection features. **Structure relocated, not lost.**
- An observer's template covers one subspace, the other, or both — a second dimension of
  expertise alongside E15's `d`.

**Prediction:** curated work is illegible to an observer reading only execution features and
legible to one whose template covers selection. That is the Duchamp phenomenon stated as a
testable claim, and it connects the framework's opening example to its expertise result.

## 2. Experiments

E1–E18 remain. New work is E19 onward.

### E19 — Does the crash survive a generous hypothesis space? **Run first.**

**Design.** Observer hypothesis space with and without `EXPLORE`, crossed with content type:
in-family human (ω = 1), foreign (ω = 0), and the nested-prior condition (human content produced
under an exploratory rather than a directed policy).

**Measure.** Engagement, goal-posterior entropy, between-observer disagreement, and the
**posterior mass assigned to EXPLORE** in each condition.

**Predicted.**
- Exploratory *human* content: EXPLORE takes high posterior mass, the observer converges, no
  crash. This is the author's argument formalized, and it predicts that low-intent human artifacts
  are *explained* rather than uncomputable.
- Foreign content: EXPLORE takes **low** posterior mass, because foreign structure does not match
  a human-shaped exploratory signature either. The crash survives.

**Falsification, and this is the one that matters.** If EXPLORE absorbs foreign content — high
posterior mass, convergence, no crash — then the V1–V3 crash was an artifact of an artificially
impoverished hypothesis space. Report it plainly and prominently. It would not invalidate the
trust-exploit or expertise results, but it would substantially weaken the central claim and the
README would have to say so.

### E20 — The overlap sweep

**Design.** ω from 0 to 1 in steps, with content goal-directed at every ω.

**Measure.** Engagement, convergence, within- and between-observer statistics, and the
misspecification signature: does the observer converge *confidently on a wrong in-family goal*
when reading foreign content?

**Predicted.** Confident fabrication peaks at **low but nonzero ω** rather than at ω = 0. Enough
in-family structure to make an explanation seem available, not enough to make it correct. If that
holds it is a novel prediction the unidentifiability model could not generate, and it is the
strongest single argument for the reframe.

This experiment subsumes the morphological-distance and hybrid-production conditions. Do not build
those separately.

### E21 — Model comparison: is the machinery necessary? **Run second.**

The question a referee will ask and nobody has answered.

**Arms:**

| arm | observer |
|---|---|
| A | full active-inference observer (current) |
| B | Bayesian inverse planning, no engagement policy (always DEEP) |
| C | heuristic label-truster: believes the declared tier, no content inference |
| D | effort-allocation heuristic: disengage on low first-observation information, no goal model |
| E | no-ToM classifier: predicts provenance from features, never infers a goal |

**Pre-register this discriminating prediction before running.** Several baselines will reproduce
*disengagement*, because disengagement is cost-benefit arithmetic and requires no theory of mind.
The signature that should require a generative model of another agent is the **simultaneous**
high-confidence / high-disagreement dissociation from E2. A heuristic can be confidently wrong; it
should not produce that specific joint pattern.

**If arm C or D reproduces the dissociation, the active inference apparatus is scaffolding rather
than mechanism.** Report it. That outcome is publishable and the failure to test for it is not.

### E22 — Reputation and the commitment device

**Design.** Named sources with encounter histories. Three studies:

1. **Defection.** A source signs honestly for `n` encounters, then defects. Measure detection
   latency, damage accumulated before detection, and recovery.
2. **Commitment device.** Two worlds, one where the 95% signature is deployed (bad actors must
   explicitly opt in to gain credibility) and one where it is not. Measure payload damage and the
   fabrication rate in each.
3. **Asymmetric cost.** Vary the reputational penalty for detected dishonesty and locate the
   threshold where honest signaling becomes the dominant strategy.

**This is Appendix B's first test.** Report it against the preprint's claims explicitly, including
where it fails to support them.

### E23 — Miscalibrated scale

**Design.** Sweep `calibration_error` from 0 to its maximum, at several coverage levels, with
both regime-aware and naive observers.

**Measure.** Payload damage, fabrication rate, and the coverage threshold from E16.

**Predicted.** There is a calibration error above which the scale is worse than no scale, because
a confidently wrong label is worse than no label — the E2 mechanism operating on honest mistakes
rather than bad actors.

**This is the most policy-relevant experiment in V4.** "How accurately must a disclosure regime be
applied before it starts doing harm" is a question standards bodies are actively facing and
nobody has a number for.

### E24 — Curation and template coverage

**Design.** Execution/selection feature partition × observer template coverage {execution,
selection, both} × artifact type {crafted, curated, foreign}.

**Predicted.** Curated artifacts are illegible to execution-only observers and legible to
selection-covering observers. Crafted artifacts show the reverse asymmetry. Foreign artifacts are
illegible to both.

### E25 — Calibration metrics (analysis only, no new simulation)

Add `brier_score` and `expected_calibration_error` to `metrics.py` and recompute across existing
E2, E17, E19, E20 outputs. Regret, accuracy, and the epistemic/pragmatic decomposition already
exist; these two are the gap. They matter because they state the fabrication result in vocabulary
an ML audience already uses: **the observer is not merely wrong, it is miscalibrated**, and the
miscalibration is induced by a label.

### E26 — Traceability as causal evidence (lower priority)

Model provenance as evidence rather than assertion: rationale traces, revision logs, edit
histories. Under Zahavian logic a revision log is a costly signal and a label is cheap talk, so
this should dominate labeling at equal coverage.

Worth noting for framing: this connects directly to content-credential standards work, which means
it has an existing audience. It is lower priority only because V4 is already large.

### E27 — The V3 residual (carryover)

V3's repaired chain missed its null narrowly (+0.00116 against a 0.001 ceiling) and left a random
walk at constant entropy. The named next step is re-running E12's N- and M-sweeps under the
deferred estimator to read the exponent. Unchanged by V4 and still open.

**Do not let V4 quietly retire E8.** It stays withheld with the `xfail(strict)` marker in place.

## 3. Regressions and nulls

**Existing N1–N15 must still pass.** Additions:

- **N16 — Boundary regression.** At ω = 1 with `EXPLORE` disabled, V4 must reproduce V2/V3 human
  results within tolerance. At ω = 0, it must reproduce the foreign/GHOST results. *Guards the
  reframe:* if V4 cannot recover its predecessors at the boundaries, the rewrite broke something
  and no V4 result is interpretable.
- **N17 — EXPLORE is not a free parameter.** Assert the constructed `sig_EXPLORE` matches the
  pre-registered file exactly. *Guards the single most tunable object in V4.* If E19's results are
  unwelcome, the temptation will be to redesign EXPLORE; this makes that visible.
- **N18 — Foreign content is goal-directed.** Assert `MI(features; foreign_goal)` under the
  foreign family exceeds a pre-registered floor. *Guards the reframe's core claim.* Foreign content
  that is not goal-informative has silently reverted to V3's goal-empty model.
- **N19 — Zero calibration error reproduces V3.** With `calibration_error = 0`, E23 must reproduce
  E16/E17. *Guards C4.*
- **N20 — Reputation null.** With honesty fixed and known, the inferred-κ observer must behave
  identically to the fixed-κ observer. *Guards C3.*

## 4. Staging and compute

V4 is large. Stage it, and treat each stage as conditional on the last.

| stage | experiments | why this order |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **E19** | decisive; if EXPLORE absorbs foreign content, everything downstream is reframed |
| 2 | **E21** | cheap, and the answer determines whether the apparatus is load-bearing |
| 3 | E20, N16–N18 | the sweep and the regressions that make it interpretable |
| 4 | E23, N19 | policy-relevant, moderate cost |
| 5 | E22, N20 | Appendix B; larger build |
| 6 | E24 | needs the feature partition, which is a nontrivial refactor |
| 7 | E25 | analysis only |
| 8 | E26, E27 | |

**Stages 1 and 2 are the whole point.** They are the two experiments that can tell the author the
framework is wrong, and they are both cheap. If compute or attention runs out after stage 2, V4
has still done its job.

Measured throughput from V3 was 331 rollouts/sec aggregate at 18 workers, and V3's cost model was
5–10× optimistic on the first estimate. Do not repeat that. Measure a single cell before
projecting a stage.

## 5. Reporting

`RESULTS_V4.md`, same discipline: written from the CSVs, non-replications reported rather than
tuned, deviations logged with evidence.

Required sections:

- **The reframe, and whether it survived.** State plainly whether V4's model reproduces V1–V3 at
  the boundaries (N16). If it does not, that comes first and everything else is provisional.
- **E19's verdict on hypothesis-space poverty.** Whether the crash survives EXPLORE. This is the
  most important paragraph in the document and it should be legible to someone who reads nothing
  else.
- **E21's verdict on necessity.** Which baselines reproduce which effects. If a heuristic
  reproduces the dissociation, say so in the first line of the section.
- **What V4 changes about the preprint.** The α ↔ opacity correspondence is no longer an
  assumption; state what the measured calibration tolerance is. Appendix B has been tested for the
  first time; state what held.
- **What remains withheld.** E8, unchanged.

## 6. What may not change

Everything in V1 §14, V2 §6, V3 §6, plus:

- **Foreign content is goal-directed, not goal-empty.** This is the reframe. A construction that
  fails N18 has reverted to the previous model.
- **EXPLORE's signature is pre-registered and asserted.** It is the most tunable object in the
  build and the one most likely to be quietly adjusted if E19 disappoints.
- **The V1–V3 results are not deleted, overwritten, or retroactively reinterpreted.** They were
  produced under a different model of synthetic content and they stand as such. V4 either
  reproduces them at its boundaries or reports why it cannot.
- **E8 stays withheld** with its `xfail(strict)` marker in place.
- **E19 and E21 must be capable of returning the unwelcome answer**, and the reporting sections
  above must be written before their results are known.

## 7. Pre-mortem

Three ways V4 fails, named now so they are recognizable later.

**The reframe silently reproduces the old model.** If `foreign_basis` overlaps the observer's
feature support, foreign content becomes unidentifiable rather than foreign, and V4 reports V3's
results in new vocabulary. N18 is the guard. Check it before trusting anything.

**EXPLORE gets tuned.** E19's unwelcome outcome is a strong incentive to adjust a signature that
has no external constraint. N17 and the pre-registration file are the guard, and the guard only
works if the file is written first.

**Scope replaces resolution.** This project's observed pattern under a disappointing result has
been to expand scope. V4 is a large expansion arriving directly after a withheld experiment and a
missed null. The mitigation is the staging in §4: stages 1 and 2 are cheap, decisive, and both
capable of saying the framework is wrong. **If the work stops after stage 2, V4 has succeeded.**
Everything past stage 3 is optional and should be treated that way.
