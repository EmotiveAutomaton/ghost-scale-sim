# V22 A3: Attention withdrawal and its costs

Does withdrawal protect uptake at matched technique? In one fixed mixture comparison with goal-bearing procedures, withdrawal reduces purpose and value memory change by 0.120 and 0.112 while technique accuracy differs by only 0.60 percentage points. Purpose-recognition loss is not established in that comparison. The separate physical learner retains unwanted uptake when useful procedures carry it. These are constructed-mechanism results; they do not establish disgust or a universal protection-with-recognition-cost tradeoff.

Sequential policies read the same fresh V22 streams. Content becomes available after step eight; procedure and flag evidence arrive earlier. Withdrawal at posterior flag thresholds 0.7 or 0.9 reduces content precision to 0.05, with an explicit technique-processing penalty. The early guard reduces every channel to 0.2. Procedure-only leaves content memory unchanged by its update rule even when its posterior infers the linked value. This is a property of the apparatus, not demonstrated psychological impermeability.

Purpose/value uptake here means absolute memory change from the initial probability 0.5. Recognition means correct modal purpose prediction; technique means the sampled later operation is correct. All are distinct from probability loss. Prefixes four and eight contain no direct content, so their zero content changes do not demonstrate protection from encountered content.

All weights 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1 are crossed. Each withdrawal or guard policy is mixed with no gate. Of 600 comparisons, 110 meet the mean technique-difference tolerance, including 24 identical no-gate controls; 490 do not. The complete matched set is published. A mean match is not a confidence-interval equivalence test, and no development-selected threshold is introduced. No withdrawal/no-gate mixture matches the pure early guard at the fixed tolerance.

At prefix 16 with goal-bearing procedures and flagged makers, compare 50% threshold-0.7 withdrawal with 25% early guard, each mixed with no gate. Withdrawal minus guard is -0.1199 [-0.1264, -0.1136] in purpose memory, -0.1121 [-0.1219, -0.1038] in value memory and +0.0060 [-0.0033, 0.0169] in technique accuracy. Recognition changes +0.00035 [-0.00446, 0.00551], so the predicted recognition cost is absent in this contrast. This row is illustrative; the report does not choose it as a promoted optimum.

Without goal-bearing procedure coupling, pure threshold-0.7 withdrawal at prefix 16 reduces flagged-maker purpose/value change from 0.428/0.333 to 0.043/0.007, while technique is 94.45% versus 95.08% without a gate. On benign makers its false-alarm rate is 31.65%, compared with 10.97% at threshold 0.9. Benign technique changes are -0.60 and 0.00 percentage points. Under coupling, benign false alarms are 25.01% and 6.71%, with technique changes -1.50 and -0.50 points. These are aggregate policy contrasts, not a randomized causal effect of firing conditional on benign status.

The E56 pure scorer independently preserves per-step accounting: the uniform early-guard uptake is exactly one fifth of no-gate uptake. This arithmetic identity is separate from sequential memory updates. It does not apply a final gate retroactively.

The protection prediction has qualified fixed-mixture support; a universal recognition-cost prediction does not. Benign false alarms and physical residual uptake remain limitations.
