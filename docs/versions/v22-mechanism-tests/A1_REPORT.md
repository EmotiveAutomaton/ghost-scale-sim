# V22 A1: Source inference and fixed trust

Does inferring a source explain uptake better than one fixed trust? In this constructed mechanism, source inference improves native property log loss by 0.102, 0.115 and 0.249 nats against trusts 0.2, 0.54 and 0.9. Steering detection rises from 13.4% without records to 100% with 32 records. The proposed competence ordering of discrepancy-curve peaks fails: with long records, competent informers peak earlier than weak informers on the tested grid. No universal trust crossover or broad architecture claim follows.

The exact reader marginalizes property, source goal, competence, agenda and noise law. A native history belongs to the current source; unrelated histories are explicitly disclosed as unrelated, and repeated observations carry public root identities. These are information privileges, not learned provenance detection. Each world contributes 256 cases, with four record lengths, two reader noise levels and all three controls.

Native paired log-loss improvements over the three fixed trusts are 0.1016 [0.0745, 0.1283], 0.1155 [0.0909, 0.1417] and 0.2491 [0.2171, 0.2820] nats. The label-only improvement is 0.1560 [0.1284, 0.1834]. The strongest tested fixed setting is 0.2; these comparisons do not exclude every possible fixed-trust value. Some individual short-history comparisons remain unresolved.

At 32 records, pooled-across-expertise competent-informer shift peaks at discrepancy 3.5 with height 2.129; the weak-informer curve keeps rising through discrepancy 6, reaching 1.096. Thus the original prediction that weak sources peak earlier is not supported by this finite apparatus. Every source/history/expertise curve is retained, including nonmonotonic curves and cells absent in some worlds. The property grid ends at 3, so a discrepancy probe as large as 6 also tests finite support and prior behavior. It cannot identify an empirical persuasion law.

Experts shift less in the pooled native curve: its maximum is 0.165 at discrepancy 3, versus novice 0.722 at the grid endpoint 6. This is descriptive pooling, not proof that every matched source cell has an earlier expert peak. Label values do not enter the exact likelihood, so changing the label leaves exact forecasts invariant by construction; detection succeeds in every tested 32-record label cell. The experiment does not discover a universal 0.54 crossover.

Correctly ignoring unrelated history improves log loss by 0.2647 [0.1618, 0.3717] over rigid same-source inference. Removing duplicates improves copied-cell loss by only 0.0030 [-0.0001, 0.0066]; native and unrelated duplicate-product controls are exactly equal to the exact reader. There is no established practical duplicate-removal gain here. Proper-score and modal-accuracy ordering can differ: in the no-record, expert, competent-informer native cell, the high-label direct reader has 0.2613 higher loss but 1.25 percentage points higher modal accuracy.

Prediction dispositions: fixed-trust inadequacy is supported for the three tested settings; long-history steering detection is supported in the finite data; the competence peak-order prediction fails; uniform earlier expert peaks and a special trust crossover remain unestablished. At 8,192 labels, 35 of 96 target strata have admitted headroom, one parity and 60 unresolved. No universal direct-reader superiority is claimed.
