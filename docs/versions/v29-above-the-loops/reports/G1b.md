# G1b: verified primary map

Does a maker's history still help when the context changes? With 16 earlier documents, own-history reading beats the typical-context reader in all six constructed settings at habit retention 1 and 0.75, in three at 0.5, and in none at 0.25 or 0. Shrinkage does not dominate both alternatives everywhere. These are descriptive constructed-method results, not evidence about real writers.

The fresh map has 90 settings and 18,000 worlds, grouped into eight independently drawn libraries per setting. No pilot cell was excluded. Sixteen-history mean advantages over the typical-context reader range from 0.295 to 3.442 nats at unchanged habits, and from -4.589 to -1.057 at independent habits. These ranges are point estimates across settings, not simultaneous intervals. The full summary carries pointwise block intervals. For independent habits at mixture 0.3 and low goal overlap, shrinkage gains 1.292 nats over own-history reading (0.819-1.764), but that does not establish dominance over the typical reader. The typical reader knows the context-B library distribution; own-history estimates include the current page, matching V28. The eighteen retained-path V28 means reproduce within 3.5e-18 without new V28 draws. Context pairing and nested histories are recorded. There is no logistic slope, lapse probability or censoring time here, so those packet texture knobs are inapplicable. Route to ToMpathy: test context transfer before treating a person's past writing as a stable habit estimate.
