# B2: verified primary map

What distinguishes a learned harm trigger from a learned safety brake? In the declared cue-correlation-0.5 worlds, their first-100-step alarm rates are 30.3% (95% block interval 28.7-32.0%) and 67.9% (67.1-68.7%). Pairwise separation takes median 4 and 3 steps respectively, reflecting the deliberately opposite starting priors. These are descriptive constructed-method results, not discoveries about human alarm learning.

The 27-cell pilot excluded the three exact rho-one fixed-rule aliases; 24 main cells contain 4,800 fresh worlds. Ten controls pass. The fixed BOTH signal saturates the sum at one, preserving the required identity when cues coincide. Learners are explicitly chosen Beta outcome trackers: TRIGGER starts with Beta(1,9) harm beliefs conditioned on forecast; BRAKE with Beta(9,1) conditioned on the safety cue. Thus early direction is fixed, and the measured alarm magnitudes are properties of those illustrative priors. Alarm costs do not alter harm. The fast pairwise result meets the packet's trigger for a separately frozen extinction/reminder follow-on, which is commissioned but not yet executed.
