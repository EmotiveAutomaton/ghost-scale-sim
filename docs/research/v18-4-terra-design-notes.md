# V18.4 research returns and design decisions

Three sequential, bounded Terra research passes were commissioned for learned
memory failures, adaptive evidence/source investigation, and prospective
compression. They did not implement or validate experiments. These notes are
research input, kept separate from the theory's evidence tables.

Before reading the literature, the local position was recorded: exact finite
predictive closure does not imply successful learning; weak linear probes do not
prove information loss; selective updating and source correction have reversals;
and old-task compression optima can tie while future loss differs.

The operating agent fetched the primary sources below. Their limited relevance
is stated explicitly; none supplies a scientific result for Ghost Scale.

- [Singh, James and Rudary, predictive state representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.4167)
  motivates observable prediction banks as candidate state descriptions. It does
  not guarantee that a neural objective will discover a sufficient state.
- [Hewitt and Liang, probe control tasks](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1275/)
  motivates selective decoder tests against randomized targets. Better decoding
  can reflect the probe's own learning, so decoder capacity is part of the claim.
- [Lake and Baroni, systematic recombination](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00350)
  motivates explicit composition holdouts. Its recurrent failures do not identify
  the cause of the failures in Ghost's much smaller supplied-law world.
- [Tishby, Pereira and Bialek, information bottleneck](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0004057)
  motivates stating the target whose information a compressed code preserves.
  A portfolio changes that target; it is additional declared purpose knowledge.
- [Adams and MacKay, online changepoint detection](https://lips.cs.princeton.edu/pdfs/adams2007changepoint.pdf)
  motivates sequential uncertainty over changes. Its independent-segment model
  differs from persistent maker roles; U1 tests finite role/hazard mixtures rather
  than claiming an implementation of the full run-length method.
- [Heckerman and Chickering, model selection criteria](https://proceedings.mlr.press/r1/heckerman97a.html)
  motivates retaining a predictive mixture as a rival to selecting one family.
  Its model-averaging results are not a guarantee under omitted laws.

The learning pass proposed count-matched support swaps, an observable-bank
objective, privileged sufficient-state distillation, a selective nonlinear decoder
ladder, and matched-update credit-assignment comparisons. L1 implements support
and query-coverage comparisons first; the rest remain separate backlog items.

The active-evidence pass proposed hazard mixtures, paid source-independence audits,
task-value versus entropy selectors, paid revision versus model averaging, and
prequential calibration. We reject its suggested use of desired stationary/change
rankings as validity gates: those are precisely the hypotheses being tested.
Its multi-hour runtime estimates are unmeasured and are not admission evidence.

The compression pass proposed prospective canonical/random/entropy tie rules,
purpose portfolios, minimax selection and prior-shift tests. P1 implements these
with minimum worst training regret, explicitly different from raw worst loss.
Future questions never select the code. Equal maximum storage remains distinct
from code entropy, and supplied-law decoding remains distinct from learned access.

Further source suggestions (not yet independently adopted) included multivariate
bottlenecks, causal rate-distortion, Blackwell experiment comparisons, active
feature acquisition and provenance truth-discovery methods. A later reviewer may
fetch them if an unresolved design question makes them useful. Repeating searches
without a concrete decision is not part of the continuing loop.

## Fourth bounded Terra pass: decoder access (20 September UTC)

Local position recorded before this return: failure on new questions does not
prove state information loss; an additional nonlinear readout tests accessibility
under additional supervision and its declared capacity. A failed readout bounds
that decoder, not every possible decoder.

The operating reviewer fetched [Hewitt and Liang's primary paper page](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1275/)
and [Pimentel et al.'s information-theoretic probing page](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.420/).
The former motivates controls against the probe's own learning; the latter frames
high-capacity extraction as a different objective from ease of decoding. Neither
establishes the outcome in this simulator. This is a difference of estimand rather
than a reason to adopt one literature framing as the local answer.

L3 adopts a fixed linear/nonlinear ladder, nested label budgets, untouched test
lineages, a question-only rival and context-stratified shuffled supervision. It
uses fresh full maker support to ask about recoverability, while the existing L1
slate tests combination holdouts. Disjoint maker combinations in every split would
conflate recoverability with combination transfer, so that research suggestion is
not adopted here. A random-feature nonlinear map makes the limited decoder family
explicit; it is not a claim of unrestricted information recovery. Randomized-target
results remain comparisons, never a requirement to achieve a desired scientific
ranking. Sample alignment controls do not reproduce the paper's word-type task.
